## C4-01 银发食堂
**Passage**
Last spring our neighborhood opened a small canteen for old people. It stands in a quiet building next to the community clinic, and the kitchen is run by four volunteers who are all over sixty. They cook lunch from ten to two every day, and a full meal costs only twelve yuan. Old people who live alone can eat there free on Wednesdays. Mrs Zhang is seventy-eight and goes three times a week. She says the food is simple but warm. "The meal is only half of it." "It is that somebody is waiting for me." The canteen also packs takeaway boxes for people who cannot walk well. On rainy days two of the volunteers carry hot soup to ten families. A board near the door lists the dishes of the day, and anyone can add a dish to tomorrow's list. Last month sixty-one dishes were suggested, and eleven of them are on the menu now. The cooks do not think of themselves as a restaurant. They say their real work is to make a noisy, bright room where old neighbors meet every day, and to notice at once when somebody does not come.
**Q1**
How much does a full meal cost at the canteen?
- A. Ten yuan.
- B. Twelve yuan.
- C. Eighteen yuan.
- D. Twenty yuan.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
What can old people who live alone do on Wednesdays?
- A. They can eat there without paying.
- B. They can have the meal sent home.
- C. They can help the volunteers cook.
- D. They can meet the four volunteers again.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
How does a new dish get onto the canteen's list?
- A. The four volunteers choose it at a meeting.
- B. The shop opposite the clinic sends it in.
- C. Anyone can add a dish to tomorrow's list.
- D. Old people order it a week before lunch.
**Answer** C
**Q4**
What does Mrs Zhang mean by "It is that somebody is waiting for me"?
- A. She hopes the canteen will cook richer food.
- B. She wants the volunteers to open in the evening.
- C. She prefers to eat her lunch at home.
- D. She values the company there more than the food.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How old people should choose cheap meals for their families.
- B. How a small team of volunteers helps old people eat together.
- C. Why some old families prefer to cook at home.
- D. How a neighborhood clinic teaches old people about food.
**Answer** B

## C4-02 周二的法律咨询台
**Passage**
Every Tuesday morning a small legal advice office opens in our community center. Two lawyers and three student volunteers work there, and anyone can walk in without an appointment. Most people come with small problems: a landlord who will not return the deposit, a second-hand phone that stops working, or a shop that keeps changing its price. Mrs Chen is seventy-two. Last spring she signed a paper she did not understand. A volunteer, Wen, sat with her for forty minutes and explained every line. He drew a simple table on paper so that she could see what she had agreed to and what she could still refuse. She kept her money, but, as she said later, she also kept her calm. The office does not fight every case. Sometimes the answer is simply that the shop is right. Last year two hundred people came, and for sixty of them the situation changed. The others left with advice that saved them from a worse mistake. The workers say the best part is not winning. It is showing people that a rule is something everybody has to follow, including the person in front of you.
**Q1**
How can people use the legal advice office?
- A. They can walk in without an appointment.
- B. They must write a letter before they come.
- C. They can come only on Friday mornings.
- D. They must pay a small fee at the door.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
How did Wen help Mrs Chen?
- A. He read the paper aloud to her family instead.
- B. He asked the shop to repeat its terms.
- C. He explained every line and drew a table.
- D. He told her to find another lawyer.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
What happens in some of the cases brought to the office?
- A. The office always takes the case to a court.
- B. The worker tells the visitor that the shop is right.
- C. The office asks the visitor to pay for the case.
- D. The worker sends a long letter to the other side.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
What can we learn about the workers from the fact that the office does not fight every case?
- A. They think that most small problems cannot be solved.
- B. They measure a good week by the cases they win.
- C. They tell every visitor what the visitor hopes to hear.
- D. They put being honest before being able to win.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How a community office helps people with small legal problems.
- B. Why a small shop keeps changing its price.
- C. How student volunteers learn to work in a law court.
- D. Why more and more people now buy second-hand phones.
**Answer** A

## C4-03 老公园的春日庙会
**Passage**
The old park in our town holds a temple fair every spring. The fair has run for more than sixty years, and the fair is still the one my grandmother knew. On the first day children carry lanterns and the opera group sings on a small stage. On the second day the shops sell dumplings, rice cakes and new-year couplets. Behind the opera stage sits Mr Wu, who draws sugar pictures with a small spoon, a hot piece of stone and melted brown sugar. He learned the skill from his father in one winter. Today he is the only one in the town who can do it, and he gives a free lesson to any child who brings an old spoon. Eight years ago Mr Wu almost stopped. Sugar became expensive, and few children wanted to stand beside a hot stone. Then a school asked him to come and show the skill to a class. One year became eight, and a young man now drives him home after the fair each evening. He says a sugar picture is not real art. It lasts about ten minutes before somebody eats it. That, he laughs, is why he keeps making it.
**Q1**
How does Mr Wu draw his sugar pictures?
- A. With thin bread on a warm flat pan.
- B. With melted sugar on a hot piece of stone.
- C. With cold paint on a sheet of glass.
- D. With hot water on a flat metal plate.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
What happened eight years ago?
- A. A school asked him to show the skill to a class.
- B. His father came back to work at the fair.
- C. The price of sugar suddenly went down that year.
- D. A young man began to drive him to the park.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
How long has the temple fair been held in the town?
- A. For about ten years.
- B. For more than forty years.
- C. For about eight years.
- D. For more than sixty years.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
What does the free lesson tell us about Mr Wu?
- A. He wants to sell his pictures to the park shops.
- B. He thinks only children of the town should learn it.
- C. He is afraid that nobody will learn the skill in time.
- D. He believes his pictures are the best of all art.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How a young painter teaches children to draw.
- B. Why an old park is closed on the first day.
- C. How a family saves money at a spring temple fair.
- D. How a man keeps an old family skill alive.
**Answer** D

## C4-04 楼道下的修理桌
**Passage**
A small table appeared under the stairs of our building six years ago. On it there were four things only: a box of screws, a pair of pliers, a small electric drill and a tin of oil. Above it somebody had written, "Bring it, and I will fix it. No money, only parts." The man behind the table is Mr Qian. He worked in a factory until he was sixty; now he opens his door at eight and closes it at five. People bring kettles, desk lamps, walking sticks and radios, and once a small wooden chair with three loose legs. Mr Qian never keeps a borrowed thing longer than one evening. Nothing at the table is free except the work. A kettle needs a new switch for four yuan; Mr Qian writes the number on a slip of paper and never says what his labour was. Last year he wrote the cost of every repair on a small slip of paper and posted all forty-one of them on the wall. The table has become a small shop without a roof. Mr Qian says he never learned to run a shop, but people come back because they have nowhere else to go.
**Q1**
What does Mr Qian ask for the work he does?
- A. Nothing at all, and the parts come free too.
- B. Four yuan for every small job at the table.
- C. A price that depends on how long the job takes.
- D. Nothing at all, but the parts must be paid for.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
What is said about the wooden chair that came to the table?
- A. It was too heavy for Mr Qian to carry alone.
- B. It was a small chair with three loose legs.
- C. It was bought new for a worker in the factory.
- D. It stayed in his room for three weeks.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
What did Mr Qian do with the forty-one small papers?
- A. He put them in a box under the stairs.
- B. He posted them on the wall.
- C. He gave them to the factory he worked for.
- D. He threw them away at the end of the year.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
What can we learn about Mr Qian?
- A. He wants to charge for his labour as well as the parts.
- B. He is afraid that too many people will come.
- C. He wants every yuan that people pay to be clear.
- D. He plans to close the table at the end of the year.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How an old worker turned a corner into a repair place.
- B. How a building decided to charge every family for repairs.
- C. Why a factory closed the workers' repair room long ago.
- D. How a factory worker became rich by selling new parts.
**Answer** A

## C4-05 图书馆下的快递点
**Passage**
Our community opened a small parcel point under the library two years ago. Workers put every parcel into a numbered locker and send a short message. People who do not like lockers can leave their name and get a phone call instead. The point is free, but it does more than store boxes. Many people here are old, and the post office machines are hard for them. So a volunteer, Uncle He, comes every afternoon to show people how to take a photo, pay for a box and ask for a refund. He uses his own phone and never takes money for it. A year ago the workers removed the small shelves for people who cannot use the lockers. Then an old woman could not fetch her parcel for six days, because she was in hospital and nobody knew her name. The shelves stayed, and today there are four of them. There are two hundred and forty lockers in the room. Last month only seven parcels stayed for more than a week, and the workers gave them to local families. A service point, they say, is also a place where nothing is thrown away.
**Q1**
How can people who do not like lockers get their boxes?
- A. They can leave their name and get a phone call.
- B. They can ask a volunteer to carry the box home.
- C. They can pay a small extra fee for the service.
- D. They can ask the post office to open the box.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
What does Uncle He do at the point?
- A. He drives boxes to the post office each afternoon.
- B. He checks the lockers for broken doors.
- C. He helps people use phone services.
- D. He writes the names of old people on the shelves.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
What did the workers do with the seven parcels?
- A. They kept the seven parcels in the lockers.
- B. They sent the seven parcels back to the post office.
- C. They put the seven parcels on the small shelves.
- D. They gave the seven parcels to local families.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
What can we learn about the workers from the last sentence?
- A. They think the point's only job is to keep boxes safe.
- B. They believe unused things should still find new owners.
- C. They hope that fewer parcels will arrive each month.
- D. They want the point to close when the library moves.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. A private shop that earns money from storing boxes.
- B. A post office that gives free boxes to hospital families.
- C. A free community point that helps people with phones and parcels.
- D. A library that teaches old people to read long messages.
**Answer** C
