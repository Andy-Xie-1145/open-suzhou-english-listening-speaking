## C-01 学校回收项目
**Passage**
Last spring, Green Middle School in Suzhou started a project called the "Green Corner". Every Friday afternoon, students stand at the school gate with two big cloth bags. They ask people walking past to put empty bottles and used paper into the bags instead of dropping them on the ground. In the first three months, the students collected 1,240 bottles and 60 kilos of paper. The school sells the paper to a paper factory in the county and sells the bottles to a small recycling shop. The money is not spent on the students themselves. It is used to buy books for the reading room in the school library. Last term the students also wrote letters to younger classes, telling them why throwing rubbish on the street is not a good habit. Now the project has 34 student volunteers, and two primary schools nearby have asked to join it next year. Mr. Wang, who teaches in one of them, says the two schools want their own pupils to learn the same habit. A teacher says the biggest change is not the number of bottles, but the way students look at the street.
**Q1**

- A. Every Monday morning.
- B. Every Friday afternoon.
- C. Every Friday morning.
- D. Every Thursday afternoon.
**Answer** B

- B. It pays for food in the reading room.
- C. It buys books for the school library.
- D. It buys bags for the younger classes.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
Why have two primary schools asked to join the project?
- A. They want to build a bigger library.
- B. They want to get money for their own school library.
- C. They want the Green Corner to move to their school.
- D. They want to put the same idea into practice.
**Answer** D
**Q4**

- A. The writer thinks it is worth supporting.

**Answer** A
**Q5**

## C-02 会修旧物的学生
**Passage**
Chen Mo is a fourteen-year-old student in Suzhou. Last year his phone fell into a bowl of soup and it never worked again. Instead of asking his parents for a new one, he took the phone to his school's old computer room and started learning how to fix it. Now he spends most Friday afternoons there with three other students. They repair old phones, small radios and toy cars, and they keep a notebook that records every problem and the way they solved it. Last term they fixed ninety-one things for free. Parents in the neighbourhood now come to the room with old radios, and Chen Mo and his friends listen to the problems, open the back cover, and try to find the broken part. Chen Mo says the work is slower than playing games, but it teaches him something games cannot. He has learnt to be patient, because a small screw can take half an hour to open. He also learnt that most broken things are not broken at all; they are simply not cleaned for years. Chen Mo hopes to become an engineer one day.

## C-03 睡得好，学得好
**Passage**
Good sleep is not something middle school students can do without. It is part of school work. During sleep, the body grows and repairs itself, and the brain sorts out everything it has learned during the day. This is why students who sleep nine hours at night usually find it easier to remember new words than students who sleep only six. Many teenagers lose sleep for the same reason: a phone. When the phone is beside the bed, "one more minute" easily becomes forty minutes, and the next morning arrives too early. A short nap of twenty minutes after lunch can help a student feel fresh again, but a long nap of two hours will make it harder to fall asleep at night. Doctors suggest three simple rules for students. First, go to bed and get up at the same time every day, even at weekends. If these times change from day to day, the body never gets used to the daily pattern, and a student can feel sleepy in class even after nine hours in bed. Second, keep the bedroom dark and quiet. Third, put the phone in another room half an hour before bed. Two weeks later, most students find that they need less time to fall asleep. The result is simple: longer sleep and better memory.
**Q1**
According to the passage, what does the body do during sleep?
- A. It becomes larger and stronger.
- B. It sends new messages to the brain.
- C. It stops working for a few hours.
- D. It grows and repairs itself.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
Why do many teenagers lose sleep at night?
- A. Because they keep using a phone beside the bed.
- B. Because they must get up very early for reading.
- C. Because their classmates call them after dinner.
- D. Because they finish their homework too late.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
A student sleeps about nine hours every night but still feels tired in class. What may be the reason?
- A. The student may read for a long time before going to bed.
- B. The student may drink too little water in the daytime.
- C. The student may keep different times for going to bed and getting up.
- D. The student may spend too much time reading books.
**Answer** C
**Q4**

- B. Takes in and arranges the things it has learned.
- C. Sends back everything to those who taught it.

**Answer** B
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?

- B. How long a student should study at night.

- D. Why phones are useful for middle school students.
**Answer** C

## C-04 社区里的书柜
**Passage**
A small wooden bookcase now stands at the entrance of Xinglong Community in Suzhou. It has no door and no glass. Anyone may take a book from it, and anyone may leave a book in it. The bookcase began in March, when Mr. Wu, a retired driver living in Building 3, carried it up from his own home and painted it blue. At first only his family used it. Then a woman on the fourth floor left four books about plants, and a boy from Building 7 left a football story. Today most of the shelves are always full, and readers take a short note and write down their names and the day they finished the book. Books stay about two weeks before they travel to the next reader. Mr. Wu checks the case every evening. If a book has been there for more than a month, he reads it and then pushes it down to the lower shelf, where a reader can still find it and take it to the next one. Last month the community office asked Mr. Wu whether more bookcases could be put up near the small garden. He said he would rather have one good bookcase than four empty ones.

## C-05 山坡跑步俱乐部
**Passage**
On the last Saturday of every month, about sixty students from three schools in Gusu District meet at the small hill behind Wenyi Park. They are members of the Hill Runners, a running club started four years ago by a sports teacher, Mr. Qian. The club now has 128 members between 12 and 16 years old. Members run the same 2.4-kilometre path every time, but they do not run alone. The club's rule is simple: everyone runs with someone of about the same speed, so that the fastest runner does not leave the slowest behind. The teachers also keep a notebook. After each run, members write their time down, and once a month the fastest times are put on the wall of the park. In May, twelve members ran the path in under twelve minutes and got free entry to the park for a month. Mr. Qian says the notebook matters more than the prizes. "If you write down your time every month, you cannot hide from it. Slowly the number gets smaller, and that is the real prize." Two new clubs in other parts of the city have asked him for his advice this year.

## C-06 慢旅行
**Passage**
Last summer holiday, Zhou Ye, a Grade 9 student from Suzhou, took a slow train to a small town in the north. The journey took about six hours, and the train stopped nine times on the way. His friends laughed at him for choosing a slow train when a fast one was available. Yet by the time the train reached the town, he had already seen everything worth seeing: long fields beside the rails, small houses with grey roofs, and a river that turned silver in the afternoon sun. Zhou Ye now says slow travel suits students. It costs less, it lets you sleep in a real bed in a small hotel instead of a train bed, and it gives the body time to slow down. He also believes the best part of a journey is the walk before the first visit. When he got off the train, he walked for twenty minutes along the river before he looked for food. His advice for other students is simple: take one slow trip this year, and take a notebook with you. Photos show what you saw, but notes show what you thought.

## C-07 怎样买得聪明
**Passage**
Shopping well is not about spending less money. It is about getting what you really need. The first rule is to write a list before you leave home, and to keep the list in your pocket. Most unplanned things in a shopping bag were bought without a need. The second rule is to look at the price per hundred grams instead of the price on the front of the packet. A packet of washing powder at a lower price often holds less powder, so it is not cheaper. The third rule is to wait one day before buying clothes. Clothes are the easiest things to buy too many of, and a coat bought in a hurry is often a coat that is never worn again. Shop assistants can also help. If you tell them how much you want to spend, they can show you the smaller and cheaper size, or a product with almost the same quality. Some shops offer a cheaper box of the same brand, and old things in good condition can be a good choice if you look at them carefully. Wise shoppers are not people who never buy anything; they are people who know why they are buying it.

- D. You should keep a list of shop assistants.

**Answer** A

## C-08 一家人的年夜饭
**Passage**
On New Year's Eve, the kitchen of Longhua Community was open from early afternoon. Twelve families came together to cook one dish each, and by six in the evening the long tables in the square were full. The idea began three years ago, when a few older neighbours found that some families ate alone on that night because their children worked far away. One family brought a big pot, another brought a table, and by dinner time everything was shared. This year the oldest cook was eighty-one years old and the youngest helper was six. Each family also wrote its dish name on a paper card and put the card beside the bowl, so guests could try a little of everything. After dinner, nobody washed up alone. People cleaned the tables together, put the bowls away, and swept the square. The twelve families have kept the plan for three years, and this year eight more families asked to join. Two neighbours said they will bring the recipe cards, so that next year the young cooks can make the same dishes themselves.
