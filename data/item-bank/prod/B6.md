## B6-01 公交车上的学生月票
**Dialogue**
Leo: Miss Wang, how much is the monthly bus pass for students?
Miss Wang: A student pass is forty yuan a month and an adult pass is eighty. The pass is good on every bus except the express one. The express costs five yuan a ride unless you buy the express add-on, which is twenty yuan a month on top.
Leo: So sixty a month, and I can use the express as well.
Miss Wang: Yes. But the card must be bought before the tenth of the month, or the office asks for a month and a half now.
Leo: Today is the eighth, and I have my old card here.
Miss Wang: Then you pay for one month only, sixty yuan. Take the card to the office and they will put the photo on.
Leo: Is the photo extra?
Miss Wang: Ten yuan, and it is free when the card is two years old.
Leo: My card is only one.
Miss Wang: Then the ten yuan is for you.
**Q1**
How much will Leo pay for the bus pass itself every month?
- A. Forty yuan.
- B. Sixty yuan.
- C. Eighty yuan.
- D. One hundred yuan.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
What does buying the card before the tenth of the month let him do?
- A. Pay for one month only, instead of a month and a half.
- B. Ride on the express bus without paying the add-on.
- C. Skip the ten yuan for the photo.
- D. Use the pass in another city.
**Answer** A

## B6-02 打印店付印班级纪念册
**Dialogue**
Kate: Ms. Wei, I want fifty copies of a book for our class, with a coloured cover.
Ms. Wei: Each copy has ninety pages inside. Inside pages are twenty cents each. Fifty copies of ninety pages is four thousand five hundred pages, so that is nine hundred yuan.
Kate: And the cover?
Ms. Wei: The coloured cover is twelve yuan for every ten copies. Fifty copies need five sets, so sixty yuan.
Kate: In all?
Ms. Wei: Nine hundred and sixty yuan. Paying does not change the price, but it does change the waiting. We print a job in three days at most, and your job goes in on Monday morning, so it will be ready on Thursday evening.
Kate: I finish work at five. Can I collect it then?
Ms. Wei: No. The counter shuts at four and we send jobs out at half past four the next day. If you come on Friday morning you can have it.
Kate: Then Friday morning.
**Q1**
How much will Kate pay for the fifty copies?
- A. Nine hundred yuan.
- B. Nine hundred and twenty yuan.
- C. Nine hundred and sixty yuan.
- D. One thousand and twenty yuan.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
When can Kate collect the copies?
- A. On Thursday at five, when she finishes work.
- B. On Friday morning, because the counter shuts at four on Thursday.
- C. On Thursday at half past four, when the job goes out.
- D. On Monday morning, when the job goes in.
**Answer** B

## B6-03 社区游泳馆的儿童票
**Dialogue**
Nina: Ms. Guo, my cousin is nine and his sister is eleven. May they use the children's pool this afternoon?
Ms. Guo: Children under fourteen, or under one metre forty, may use the children's pool. They may.
Nina: What time does it close?
Ms. Guo: The children's pool closes at half past five, because the big pool takes the water for cleaning after that. We open at one, and you must be out of the water at four thirty at the latest. The last hour is for lessons.
Nina: So we have three and a half hours.
Ms. Guo: Yes. The fee is eight yuan for an adult and five yuan for a child. A child under one metre twenty swims free, but neither of yours is that small.
Nina: Then it is four of us, two adults and two children.
Ms. Guo: Two times eight and two times five, twenty-six yuan. Pay at the desk; the pool is free for the first hour on Mondays, but today is Wednesday.
Nina: Fine. We will be out at four thirty.
**Q1**
How much will the four of them pay for the afternoon?
- A. Twenty-six yuan.
- B. Twenty yuan.
- C. Thirty-two yuan.
- D. Forty yuan.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
What is the latest time they may be in the water?
- A. At four o'clock.
- B. At half past three.
- C. At five o'clock.
- D. At four thirty.
**Answer** D

## B6-04 寄养店寄养一只猫
**Dialogue**
Jack: Ms. Luo, can you look after my cat for three days while I go to my cousin's wedding?
Ms. Luo: Yes. The rate is sixty yuan a day, and the last day is free if you collect before noon.
Jack: I shall collect at ten, then. And food?
Ms. Luo: Food is charged for every day you are away: one tin a day at twelve yuan. So three tins.
Jack: Then the room is two charged days. Is that all?
Ms. Luo: Yes. Two days at sixty and three tins at twelve. About the cat herself, we open the tin at eight in the morning and again at six in the evening, and the boy next door lets her out into our yard at noon, but he gives her nothing to eat then.
Jack: Does she need the yard locked at night?
Ms. Luo: The yard gate is locked at nine, and we draw the curtains at eight so that she is not sitting at the window.
**Q1**
How much will Jack pay for the three days in all?
- A. One hundred and twenty yuan.
- B. One hundred and forty-four yuan.
- C. One hundred and thirty-two yuan.
- D. One hundred and fifty-six yuan.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
How many times a day is the cat fed at the shop?
- A. Once a day.
- B. Three times a day.
- C. Twice a day.
- D. Four times a day.
**Answer** C

## B6-05 快递柜里的三个包裹
**Dialogue**
Emma: Dad, three parcels came for me in the locker, but my code has stopped working.
Dad: A code works for three days. The first day is free. On the second day you pay one yuan and on the third day two yuan.
Emma: One parcel came on Monday, one on Wednesday, and the small one today.
Dad: Then the Monday parcel is on its third day, the Wednesday one on its second, and today's is on its first.
Emma: Two yuan, one yuan and nothing. Three yuan.
Dad: Exactly. But the locker takes no coins, so bring a card. The parcel room round the corner closes at half past eight, and it is quarter past six now.
Emma: Then we have an hour and a quarter.
Dad: Two hours and a quarter, till half past eight. Take the stair, not the lift; the lift is slow after six.
**Q1**
How much will the family pay in total to take out the three parcels?
- A. Three yuan.
- B. Two yuan.
- C. Four yuan.
- D. One yuan.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
How much time do they have before the parcel room closes?
- A. One hour and a quarter.
- B. Two hours and a quarter.
- C. Two hours.
- D. One hour.
**Answer** B

## B6-06 社区木工角做一只小鸟食盒
**Dialogue**
Girl: Mr. Wu, I would like to make a bird box at the community wood shop. How much board do I need?
Mr. Wu: The box is made of six pieces, and every piece is twenty long, twenty wide and one thick. I sell boards one metre long and twenty wide, and each metre costs eight yuan.
Girl: So one metre gives me five pieces.
Mr. Wu: It does, and you need six, so two metres. You may take the four pieces left over home.
Girl: How long will it take me?
Mr. Wu: Cutting and nailing take twenty-five minutes if you work alone. The shop closes at five, and it is half past three now.
Girl: Then I can finish before you shut.
Mr. Wu: You can cut and nail, but the glue needs twenty minutes more, and I do not let glue dry inside. Wait in the yard.
Girl: All right. When may I take it home?
Mr. Wu: At a quarter past four, and not before, or the box falls apart on the walk.
**Q1**
How many metres of board must the girl buy?
- A. One metre.
- B. Three metres.
- C. Six metres.
- D. Two metres.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
At about what time can she take the box home?
- A. At half past three.
- B. At four o'clock.
- C. At a quarter past four.
- D. At five o'clock.
**Answer** C

## B6-07 学校食堂的午餐票
**Dialogue**
Kate: Mr. Deng, how much does a lunch ticket cost?
Mr. Deng: A single ticket is five yuan and it is good for one lunch. A month of twenty school days is a hundred yuan, so the month costs the same as twenty single tickets. We do not sell single tickets on Fridays, because the kitchen is busy.
Kate: I need eleven days; I am away for nine.
Mr. Deng: Then eleven tickets, fifty-five yuan. The canteen also closes an hour early on the last Friday of the month, but you are not here then.
Kate: I finish at ten past seven. Can I buy a ticket then?
Mr. Deng: Yes, but on a Tuesday the queue is about fifteen minutes.
Kate: All right, I shall come at seven.
**Q1**
How much will Kate pay for eleven lunches?
- A. Fifty yuan.
- B. Ninety-one yuan.
- C. Fifty-five yuan.
- D. One hundred yuan.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
About how long must she wait at the window on a Tuesday?
- A. About fifteen minutes.
- B. About thirty minutes.
- C. About fifty minutes.
- D. About two hours.
**Answer** A

## B6-08 理发店门口的三个人
**Dialogue**
Auntie Qiu: Mr. Sun, I have been waiting since one o'clock, and I need my hair done before I buy the cake.
Mr. Sun: I am sorry. The young man in my chair started at one and needs thirty minutes, so your turn begins at half past one.
Auntie Qiu: How long do I take?
Mr. Sun: You said thirty-five minutes last week. Half past one and thirty-five is five past two. The shop next door sells cakes and shuts at half past four, so you have time.
The girl: Is my turn after her?
Mr. Sun: Yes, and you told me forty minutes. That takes you from five past two to twenty-five to three. Your train is at two, so it will be late.
The girl: Then I shall take the four o'clock one.
Mr. Sun: Then I can finish you by half past four and I shut at five.
**Q1**
About what time will Auntie Qiu's hair be finished?
- A. At half past one.
- B. At five past two.
- C. At two o'clock.
- D. At ten past two.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
Why will the girl not catch her two o'clock train?
- A. Mr. Sun shuts the shop at two o'clock.
- B. She is behind the young man in the chair.
- C. Auntie Qiu bought the girl's ticket.
- D. She will have forty minutes of her own, and only after Auntie Qiu.
**Answer** D
