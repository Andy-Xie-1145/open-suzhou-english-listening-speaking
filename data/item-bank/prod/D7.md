# D7 类 · 记录簿与单据类表格填空（10 份）

> 来源：并行子任务产出｜共 10 份 / 30 空｜每份词库 5 个短语，恰好 2 个为干扰项
> 全部为仿写素材，非真题。本题型（材料 + 表格 + 挖空 + 从词库选短语填入）依据用户记忆生成，**结构未经官方证实**。
> 本批人物与称谓口径：叙述与对话一律用英文名，称呼只用 Miss / Ms. 与 Mr. / Uncle / Auntie；**说话人标签全批共 20 种**（D7-01/03/05/07/10 为四人对谈，各含 4 个不同标签；D7-02/04/06/08/09 为叙述性材料，不带说话人标注）。
> 本批表格列数口径：10 份统一为 **5 列**，日期一律「日在前、月在后」，数量与时间列的写法在每份材料正文中另行说明。
> 本批题号：D7-01 至 D7-10，主题与 D-batch1、D4、D5、D6 均不重复。

## D7-01 公共自行车骑行记录
**Dialogue**
Ms. He: Good morning. The station keeps a paper book for every ride, and I fill it in while you are still on the road.
Leo: I took a bike at ten past seven and brought it back at five to eight. What will you write?
Ms. He: Your card number, the hour you took it, the hour you brought it back, and the minutes. Forty-five minutes, so one yuan.
Auntie Tang: Mine was longer. My card is H-3182 and I went out at a quarter past seven and came back at five past nine.
Ms. He: That is one hour and fifty minutes, and the book writes that out in full.
Uncle Ma: My card is H-2088. Out at twenty to eight, back at half past eight.
Ms. He: Fifty minutes. The two hour columns are written as hh:mm and the last column is written in words.
Leo: Do you keep the card numbers too?
Ms. He: All three. Nothing went in on the twelfth, so there is no line for the twelfth.
**Table**
| Rider | Card number | Out at | Back at | Time used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leo | H-2071 | ① | 07:55 | 45 minutes |
| Auntie Tang | H-3182 | 07:15 | ② | 1 hour 50 minutes |
| Uncle Ma | H-2088 | 07:40 | ③ | 50 minutes |
**Word Bank**
A. 07:10
B. 09:40
C. 09:05
D. 07:55
E. 08:30
**Answers**
① A　② C　③ E

## D7-02 学校食堂午餐预订名单
**Passage**
The school canteen keeps the lunch list in the window office, and Ms. Ma writes a new one at the start of every term. She began the latest list on the first Monday with thirty-one names on it, although only three are used most days. A booking is made by the week, and the second column is the days the student asked for, not the days she actually ate. A hot box is booked on days when the kitchen cooks meat and a cold box on the others, and nobody changes a box once it is booked. The window shuts at twelve forty-five, and a student who comes later finds her box on a shelf at the back marked missed. This term the first student on the list is Lin Yu, who took every day she booked and never came late. The second is Zhao Min, who booked fourteen days and was away once for a music lesson. The third is Wu Lei, who booked fifteen days and was away twice; he asked for a hot box because he has a trial in the afternoon and says a cold box does not keep till two.
**Table**
| Name | Days booked | Box | Picked up at | Days missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | 12 | a cold box | 12:30 | 0 |
| Zhao Min | ② | a cold box | 12:40 | 1 |
| Wu Lei | 15 | ③ | 12:25 | 2 |
**Word Bank**
A. a hot box
B. Lin Yu
C. a cold box
D. fourteen days
E. two days
**Answers**
① B　② D　③ A

## D7-03 居民垃圾分类投放记录
**Dialogue**
Mr. Yuan: I am the collector. One line for every bin that is used, and no line at all for a bin that stands empty. The day column is written with the day first and the month after it, never the year, and the weight column keeps one decimal place.
Ms. Yan: My kitchen bin was full on the thirteenth. The book has the date, but the middle column is empty.
Mr. Yuan: The kitchen bin takes leftover food and bones. Never the packaging.
Ms. Yan: And it was six and a half kilos that day.
Mr. Yuan: I have it. Uncle Liang's garden bin was the next day, on the fourteenth, three kilos of garden waste.
Uncle Liang: That is right. I cut the hedge in that week.
Auntie Wen: Mine was two days after that, on the sixteenth. Two point two kilos of packaging, and that has to go in the paper bin, because the packaging was a box.
Mr. Yuan: Correct. The glass bin is only for bottles and jars, and nobody has used it this month.
Ms. Yan: And on the twelfth?
Mr. Yuan: Nothing at all, so there is no line for it.
**Table**
| Day | Bin | Waste put in | Weight (kg) | Who carried it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | kitchen | ② | 6.5 | Ms. Yan |
| 14 October | garden | garden waste | 3.0 | Uncle Liang |
| 16 October | ③ | packaging | 2.2 | Auntie Wen |
**Word Bank**
A. 12 October
B. a paper bin
C. 13 October
D. a glass bin
E. leftover food
**Answers**
① C　② E　③ B

## D7-04 社区体检的分项结果
**Passage**
The community clinic runs one free check every June for the people on its list, and this year it was on the fourteenth and the fifteenth. The nurse measures height, weight and the left eye only, because the right eye was done the year before and the clinic never does the same eye twice. All the eye numbers are written in the same way, one point after the point, so eight tenths is 0.8 and never 8 or 80. The advice is worked out from the left eye and from nothing else. Nine tenths or better means fit for the exercise class. Eight tenths means see the doctor within a month. Anything below seven tenths means see the doctor at once, and the nurse writes nothing else in that column. Lin Yu came on the first day; her height was 158 and her left eye was 0.6. Chen Lan came on the first day as well, and her eye was 0.9. Mr. Zhou came on the second day, and his eye was 0.8.
**Table**
| Name | Height (cm) | Weight (kg) | Left eye | Advice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lin Yu | 158 | 52 | 0.6 | ① |
| Chen Lan | 162 | 55 | 0.9 | ② |
| Mr. Zhou | 170 | 74 | 0.8 | ③ |
**Word Bank**
A. fit for the exercise class
B. see the doctor within a week
C. see the doctor within a month
D. see the doctor at once
E. no advice needed
**Answers**
① D　② A　③ C

## D7-05 社区烘焙坊的原料领用单
**Dialogue**
Mr. Fu: I open the bakery at five and I write out what each of you takes. Three lines this week.
Ms. Du: Monday was mine. I took twelve kilos of strong flour for the bread that sells on Tuesdays.
Mr. Fu: Strong flour, twelve kilos. You do not take the soft flour?
Ms. Du: Never. The soft flour is mine to buy if I want it, and I did not.
Mr. Wei: My line is Tuesday. Ten kilos of the same flour, because I make the flat breads.
Mr. Fu: Ten kilos, on the second day.
Auntie Song: Mine is the third day. Eight kilos of milk powder, and that is all I took.
Mr. Fu: Both quantity columns are written to one decimal place. The last column is what is left in the store at closing, and the three left-over figures this week are 1.5, 2.0 and 0.5.
Ms. Du: In which order do those go down?
Mr. Fu: In the order of the days.
**Table**
| Day | What was taken | How much (kg) | Who took it | Left over (kg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | ① | 12.0 | Ms. Du | 1.5 |
| Tuesday | strong flour | ② | Mr. Wei | 2.0 |
| Wednesday | ③ | 8.0 | Auntie Song | 0.5 |
**Word Bank**
A. 0.5
B. 10.0
C. 2.0
D. milk powder
E. strong flour
**Answers**
① E　② B　③ D

## D7-06 剧社道具借领单
**Passage**
The school drama club keeps a book of borrowed props, and Auntie Hong writes a line whenever something leaves the storeroom. She began a new page on the first of March, and the date column is written with the day first and the month after it, never the year. There are three lines on the page. The first two are for the spring play on the twentieth; the third is for the puppet show. Lin Yu plays the captain, so she took the two wooden swords on the third of March and they are due back on the twelfth, since the swords are needed for the dress rehearsal on the thirteenth. Wu Lei is the king and borrowed one paper crown on the sixth of March; it is the only crown there is, and it comes back on the nineteenth, the day before the play. Zhao Min looks after the lanterns and took four of them on the sixth of March as well. The paper lanterns are the only props the club buys again each year, so they come back on the twelfth with the swords. She writes nothing about the twenty-sixth of March, because nothing was taken that day.
**Table**
| Borrowed on | Item | Number | Who took it | Back on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | a wooden sword | 2 | Lin Yu | 12 March |
| 6 March | ② | 1 | Wu Lei | 19 March |
| 6 March | a paper lantern | ③ | Zhao Min | 12 March |
**Word Bank**
A. four
B. 3 March
C. a wooden shield
D. 26 March
E. a paper crown
**Answers**
① B　② E　③ A

## D7-07 出租车发票与车费

Ms. Ma: Monday was mine. I was at the office building until half past four and at the hospital by twenty to five.

Ms. Ma: Thirty-eight yuan. That is what the book says.
Mr. Fan: Tuesday was Uncle Ren's. He went from the school to the airport in the afternoon.
Uncle Ren: Twenty-five kilometres, and I paid fifty-two.

## D7-08 社区棋类活动的对局记录
**Passage**
The chess and go room above the community office opens on Friday evenings, and Uncle Qian keeps the results book. He writes one line for every finished game, and he writes the two players in the order black and white, whatever their age. He began a new page on the first Friday of the month, and only three lines are on it so far, because a game that is not finished is not written down. In the first game Mr. Guan played Uncle Liang at Chinese chess and black won; Mr. Guan always plays black and Uncle Liang always plays white. The second game was go, and Ms. Su played Mr. Zhao; Ms. Su plays white and Mr. Zhao plays black, and white won by six stones. The third game was Chinese chess again, and Auntie Wen played Mr. Gao; Auntie Wen plays black, and this time black won again. Uncle Qian writes the result in words and never as one and nought, and he does not record the number of moves.

| 2 | Go | Mr. Zhao | ② | White won |
| 3 | Chinese chess | Auntie Wen | Mr. Gao | ③ |
**Word Bank**
A. Mr. Zhao
B. Mr. Guan
C. Ms. Su

## D7-09 农场鸡舍的每日记录
**Passage**
Uncle Qian looks after the hens at the farm, and he writes one line a day for every coop in the book by the feed bin. The feed column is written to one decimal place, because half a kilo is the smallest amount he measures. There are two coops: the large one, which he calls the yard, and the small one, which he calls the porch. On the Monday of that week the yard birds ate nine kilos of feed and laid twenty-four eggs, and he collected every one of them. On Tuesday the porch birds ate six kilos and laid nineteen, and one of those eggs was broken on the stone. On Wednesday the yard birds ate eight kilos and laid twenty-seven, and he collected every one of those as well. He counts the eggs twice, once in the morning and once at four, and the number in the book is always the second count. The book has three lines for that week and no line at all for Sunday, because he shuts the sheds on Sunday.
**Table**
| Day | Coop | Feed (kg) | Eggs | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | the yard | 9.0 | 24 | all collected |
| Tuesday | ① | ② | 19 | one broken |
| Wednesday | the yard | 8.0 | ③ | all collected |
**Word Bank**
A. the porch
B. 27
C. 22
D. 8.0
E. 6.0
**Answers**
① A　② E　③ B

## D7-10 小镇燃气安检记录
**Dialogue**
Mr. Guo: I check the gas on every house on Green Street and Willow Lane, and I write one line for each door. The date column is written day first, month after, never the year.
Ms. Pei: Number twelve was on the third of May. The result column is empty in your book.
Mr. Guo: The pipe there is older than the meter. Too old a pipe, and the action column says a new pipe.
Ms. Pei: The gas office man said the same.
Mr. Guo: It is what I write when a pipe is past its date. Nothing is decided on the spot.
Uncle Shen: Number fourteen was on the same day and it was all right.
Mr. Guo: Yes. It passes, and the action column is nothing to do.
Uncle Shen: Then why is there a line at all?
Mr. Guo: Because the book has a line for every door, good or bad. Willow Lane number eight was on the tenth of May and passed as well.
Miss Qian: Then what does its action column say?
Mr. Guo: A second visit, because the door number was not readable from the lane. Nothing is wrong.
**Table**
| Street | House | Day | Result | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green Street | 12 | 3 May | ① | a new pipe |
| Green Street | 14 | 3 May | pass | ② |
| Willow Lane | 8 | 10 May | ③ | a second visit |
**Word Bank**
A. a new meter
B. a new valve
C. too old a pipe
D. nothing to do
E. pass
**Answers**
① C　② D　③ E
