## C8-01 山里的护林员
**Passage**
Wei Ming has walked the same eleven kilometres of fire line for twenty-two years, and he knows it by the shape rather than by the map. He goes up in the last week of March, when the ground is still hard, and comes down in the second week of November, before the snow. In between he has a bicycle and a small tent, and he sleeps in the hut on the fourth kilometre most weeks. The hut has a stove and a bed and a window that looks south, and he has painted the window frame twice. He does not go up on a day when there is no need; the station in the village rings him if a column of smoke is seen from the road. In twenty-two years the station has rung nine times, and seven of those were cooking fires on a farm. He keeps a list of the dates and the wind direction, and he reads the list before he goes up each year. The list is his own and nobody has ever asked to see it. He says the best week is the last one, in November, because the trees are bare and a fire can be seen from a very long way off.
**Q1**
For how long does Wei Ming stay on the mountain each year?
- A. Twenty-two years.
- B. From the last week of March to the first week of November.
- C. From the last week of March to the second week of November.
- D. From the second week of November to the last week of March.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
Why does he come down in November?
- A. Because the ground is hard in March and the snow comes in November.
- B. Because the station rings him in November.
- C. Because the window of the little hut really does look south that way.
- D. Because the trees are bare in November.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
What can we learn about the nine times the station has rung?
- A. Seven of them were real fires in the forest that anyone could see for miles.
- B. Two of them were cooking fires on a farm.
- C. Each ring led to a new column of smoke.
- D. Most were not forest fires; seven of the nine were cooking fires on a farm.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
What does he do with the list of dates and wind directions?
- A. He gives a copy to the village station every year.
- B. He reads it before he goes up each year.
- C. He pins it to the wall of the hut.
- D. He hands it over when he retires.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
Why does he call the last week the best one?
- A. Because he has no work to do then, and the window frame has already been painted twice.
- B. Because the hut has just been painted, and he sleeps in the hut on the fourth kilometre.
- C. Because the snow has not come yet, and the wind direction is on the list he reads.
- D. Because the trees are bare and a fire can be seen from a very long way off.
**Answer** D

## C8-02 印刷厂里写春联的师傅
**Passage**
Mr. Xu has written the red paper for the door of his street since he was twenty-six, and the shop has printed the rest. A pair is written twice: once in the morning, in a hard pencil, and once in the afternoon, in ink, when the paper has dried. He writes the fourteen characters of a long pair, and the small pair is nine. Ink is expensive, so the first attempt is made in pencil and only the good one is inked; about two in five of the long pairs are thrown away. The paper comes from a warehouse four streets away and is cut on a Friday. He keeps every one of the first characters in a box, sorted by shape, because the first character is what people stop at. In fifty years he has written about nine thousand pairs. He can no longer write the brush character for wind, and he says he does not mind, because people do not look at the fourth character from the left; they look at the first one. Two years ago the printing shop asked him to train a boy. The boy left after four months.
**Q1**
Why is the first attempt made in pencil?
- A. Because the shop has no red ink.
- B. Because ink is expensive, so only the good one is inked.
- C. Because the paper is really only ever cut on a Friday.
- D. Because the boy cannot use a brush.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
Why does Mr. Xu keep the first characters in a box?
- A. Because the printing shop really needs them all for its type.
- B. Because the character for wind is the hardest one.
- C. Because he wants to give them away.
- D. Because the first character is the one people stop at.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
How many characters are there in a long pair of red paper?
- A. Fourteen.
- B. Nine.
- C. Forty.
- D. Five.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What happened when the shop asked him to train a boy two years ago?
- A. The boy stayed for four years.
- B. The boy would not learn to write in pencil.
- C. The boy left after four months.
- D. The shop gave up the idea of training anyone.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why red paper is only ever cut on a Friday at a warehouse four streets away.
- B. How one man has written the door paper of his street for fifty years.
- C. What the two different inks used in one day are.
- D. Why people do not look at the fourth character.
**Answer** B

## C8-03 周末才开门的修理铺
**Passage**
The repair shop on Pine Street opens on Saturday and Sunday only, and has done for twenty-nine years. Zhou Bing, who runs it, is seventy-one. He was a factory mechanic for thirty-two years and he took the week off on Friday nights, which is when he did the repairs that could not wait: a kettle lead, a lamp plug, a chair leg. When the factory closed he kept only that day. He fixes four things a week, and there is no charge for the first half hour of looking at a thing. He keeps a tin of parts behind the counter and about a third of the jobs end with him using something from that tin. People bring him things from three towns, because the shop next door with a proper counter does not open at all in August. He says a job that is not finished in an hour is not his job, and he sends those to the shop with the counter. His hands are slow now. A kettle takes him forty minutes; it took him twenty-five when he was fifty.
**Q1**
Why did Zhou Bing keep Friday night after the factory closed?
- A. Because the shop next door opens on Friday, and it has opened for twenty-nine years.
- B. Because his hands are slow, and a kettle takes him forty minutes instead of twenty-five.
- C. Because the factory asked him to, and he was a factory mechanic for thirty-two years.
- D. Because Friday night was the day he had always done repairs that could not wait.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
How many things does he fix in a week?
- A. About thirty.
- B. One.
- C. Four.
- D. Ten.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
Why do people bring him things from three towns?
- A. Because his shop opens at the weekend and the shop next door does not open at all in August.
- B. Because the tin behind his counter has more parts, and about a third of his jobs end that way.
- C. Because his prices are lower than any other shop, and he has no charge for the first half hour.
- D. Because the factory used to send its own workers to him, and he was a mechanic for thirty-two years.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What does he do with a job that is not finished in an hour?
- A. He sends it to the shop with the counter.
- B. He keeps it until the next Saturday.
- C. He charges for the first half hour only.
- D. He asks the customer to come back in August.
**Answer** A
**Q5**
What does the passage tell us about Zhou Bing's hands?
- A. They were always slow, even when he was fifty.
- B. They are no longer able to hold a tool.
- C. A kettle takes him forty minutes now, and twenty-five when he was fifty.
- D. They are really the reason he now works only four jobs a week.
**Answer** C

## C8-04 给字典编例句的中学生
**Passage**
Lin Yu is sixteen and has written an example sentence for every word in one dictionary, from the first page to the last. She started in September with a rule: a sentence must be one she has actually said out loud, and it must not need a dictionary of its own. The dictionary has about eighteen thousand head words and eleven hundred of them are names of things she has never seen. For those she leaves the line empty and writes the page number instead, so she can come back. Her English teacher said the work was a waste of paper and then, in January, set the same task for a class of forty. Twenty-six of them finished. She does not write in the margins, because a dictionary with more words in it is harder to use, not easier. She keeps the finished pages in order in a box file, and she has used her own sentences more than once when she has forgotten a word. She says the dictionary was not written for her and she is writing her own.
**Q1**
What rule did Lin Yu start with in September?
- A. A sentence must be one she has actually said out loud and must not need a dictionary of its own.
- B. Every head word must have a sentence, with no exceptions at all, and she keeps the finished pages in order.
- C. Sentences must be written in the margins, and she keeps all the finished pages in order in a box file.
- D. Sentences must be at least ten words long, and she began all of this herself in September with a rule.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
What does she do with a head word for a thing she has never seen?
- A. She invents a sentence for it.
- B. She leaves the line empty and writes the page number.
- C. She crosses the word out.
- D. She copies the whole sentence straight out of another dictionary.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
Why does she not write in the margins?
- A. Because the margins are too narrow, and her own box file would not shut.
- B. Because her teacher told her not to, and she keeps her own sentences anyway.
- C. Because a dictionary with more words in it is harder to use, not easier.
- D. Because the pages would no longer close, and she keeps the pages in order.
**Answer** C
**Q4**
What happened after her English teacher set the same task for the class?
- A. All forty students finished it.
- B. Her teacher stopped the exercise in January.
- C. Only Lin Yu's pages were kept.
- D. Twenty-six of the forty finished it.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What does she mean by saying that the dictionary was not written for her?
- A. She is writing a set of examples that she can use herself.
- B. She only thinks that the dictionary has far too many head words.
- C. She cannot read the print in the dictionary.
- D. She wants a different dictionary for the class.
**Answer** A

## C8-05 在屋顶上孵蛋的母鸡
**Passage**
The hen on the roof of the building at number 9 has laid fourteen eggs in two years, and nobody in the block can say who owns her. She came there in the way such birds come, which is that nobody is looking, and she has stayed on the flat top of the stair well ever since. Her eggs are not all in one place; she moves them. In March she was on the water tank, in June she was behind the pipe, and in November she was in the corner by the door where the sun comes first. On the first cold morning she covers them and uncovers them the next day, which is more than most people manage with a single egg. She has raised four young from the fourteen. The block has asked the council twice about her and been told twice that she is not a nuisance. Auntie Guo, who lives on the third floor and cannot climb, has a folding chair carried up for her once a week in the warm months, and she goes up in her brother's lift.
**Q1**
How many eggs has the hen laid in two years?
- A. Four.
- B. Nine.
- C. Fourteen.
- D. Two.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
Why are the eggs not all in one place?
- A. Because the block keeps moving her.
- B. Because the sun damages them in one spot.
- C. Because the council asked her to move them.
- D. Because she moves them herself.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
What happens on the first cold morning?
- A. She lays a new egg.
- B. She covers the eggs and uncovers them the next day.
- C. She really moves to the corner by the door that gets the sun.
- D. Auntie Guo carries her a chair.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
What has the council said about the hen?
- A. Twice that she is not a nuisance.
- B. The council said once that she must be moved.
- C. Nothing at all.
- D. That Auntie Guo may keep her.
**Answer** A
**Q5**
What does the passage tell us about Auntie Guo?
- A. That she lives on the top floor, and she goes up in her brother's lift once a week too.
- B. That a chair is carried up for her twice a week, and she carries it up herself in winter.
- C. That she cannot climb, and a chair is carried up for her once a week in the warm months.
- D. That she takes the eggs away each week, and the corner by the door is where the sun comes.
**Answer** C

## C8-06 存了三十年邮票的看门人
**Passage**
Uncle Hong has been the gatekeeper at the middle school since he left it as a boy, and for thirty years he collected stamps in the pocket book he carried on the night rounds. He never bought a rare one. He says a rare stamp needs money and he never had money, but a stamp that has been through a school is free and there are more of those than people think. He has about eleven thousand, in nine pocket books, and they are all ordinary: festivals, cities, birds, and a great many with a postmark nobody can read. Once a year, in the last week of August, he puts the books on the desk in the duty room and the children may look, but nobody may take one. Eleven years ago a boy slipped one into his pocket and Uncle Hong said nothing to him and said nothing to the boy's father, and the boy came back three weeks later with it. In his last year at the gate he gave the whole lot to the school library in a box, and asked for one thing: that they be kept in the order he had them in.
**Q1**
Why did Uncle Hong never buy a rare stamp?
- A. Because he never really knew what a rare stamp was at all.
- B. Because a rare stamp needs money and he never had money.
- C. Because his pocket books were already full.
- D. Because the school forbade it.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
Where did most of his stamps come from?
- A. From school post that came free.
- B. From an ordinary little shop somewhere in the city.
- C. From other collectors.
- D. From his own wages.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
What happened eleven years ago?
- A. A boy was then really caught taking a whole little pocket book of old stamps.
- B. The boy's father came to the gate.
- C. A boy slipped a stamp into his pocket and gave it back three weeks later.
- D. The children asked to keep the stamps.
**Answer** C
**Q4**
When may the children look at the books?
- A. Every evening after the last round.
- B. At any time, but they may not take one.
- C. Only really in the last week of the middle school year.
- D. In the last week of August, and nobody may take one.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What did he ask for when he gave the stamps to the library?
- A. That they be kept in the order he had them in.
- B. That the library buy a new box.
- C. That his own name be put right on all the books.
- D. That the children may take them.
**Answer** A
