## C6-01 钟表铺的学徒第一年
**Passage**
Mr. Qiao's watch shop is four steps wide and open from eight to six. He took on an apprentice last March, a boy of fifteen called Tian Yu. In the first month the boy only swept the floor and carried watches to the back room, because Mr. Qiao wanted him to hear a watch before he touched one. There are about two hundred watches in the shop and none of them is cheap; the cheapest is a thirty-yuan alarm clock, and the dearest one in the case is a five-hundred-yuan table clock that has stood on that shelf for eleven years. Tian Yu was surprised that nobody had asked about that one. Mr. Qiao said that people do not buy the dearest thing in a shop; they buy the one they can explain to their family. Now the boy changes a battery in ten minutes and a glass in twenty-five, and Mr. Qiao lets him do both alone. The shop shuts for an hour at noon and they eat at the counter. Last month Tian Yu was left alone at the counter for fifty minutes, and he wrote down the time of every customer who came in. Mr. Qiao read the list that evening and said nothing. He has said nothing about it since.
**Q1**
What was Tian Yu allowed to do in his first month?
- A. Sell watches across the counter to customers.
- B. Sweep the floor and carry watches to the back room.
- C. Change the glass in a watch by himself.
- D. Read the price list aloud to the customers in the shop.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
How much does the dearest watch in the shop cost?
- A. Thirty yuan.
- B. A hundred and fifty yuan.
- C. Three hundred yuan.
- D. Five hundred yuan.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
How long does it take Tian Yu to change a glass now?
- A. Twenty-five minutes.
- B. Ten minutes.
- C. Half an hour.
- D. A quarter of an hour.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What did Mr. Qiao do after he read the list that evening?
- A. He asked Tian Yu to write the list again.
- B. He raised the prices in the shop.
- C. He said nothing, and has said nothing since.
- D. He told Tian Yu to stop writing down the time.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why a small watch shop never sells its dearest watch.
- B. How a boy of fifteen learned to be a watchmaker.
- C. What Mr. Qiao says about the way people choose a watch.
- D. How many watches there are in a shop four steps wide.
**Answer** B

## C6-02 屋顶上的三箱蜜蜂
**Passage**
Fang Yi keeps bees on the roof of a twelve-storey block for six summers. Her father started with one box; she has three, and the third is the one that earns the money. A hive needs about four square metres of clear air around it, and the roof of a lift machine room is the only flat place far enough from the laundry lines. She goes up at six in the morning, before the heat, and again at nine at night. In between she works in an office eleven floors down. She never opens a box on a day when the wind is over level three, and she has lost two swarms that way. Honey is taken once a year, in the last week of July. That year she pulled ninety kilograms from the three boxes. Her father used to say that honey is not made by the bees but by the weather, and Fang Yi now writes the weather down before the honey. The neighbours complained once, in the second year, and she gave the first floor two jars. Now there is a waiting list, and the list has eleven names on it. The third box is the one that overwinters well; the other two are hers to take or leave.
**Q1**
Why does Fang Yi choose the roof of the lift machine room?
- A. Because the laundry lines there are the driest.
- B. Because the lift machine room is the only place with water.
- C. Because it is the only flat place with enough clear air.
- D. Because the other roofs are too near the office windows.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
When does Fang Yi open the boxes?
- A. At six in the morning and again at nine at night.
- B. Only at nine at night, never in the morning.
- C. At six in the morning only, well before the summer heat.
- D. At any time when the wind is under level three.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
What happened when the wind was over level three?
- A. The neighbours complained about the bees.
- B. The office asked her to stop opening boxes.
- C. She wrote the weather instead of the honey.
- D. She lost two swarms.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
How many names are on the waiting list?
- A. Two.
- B. Eleven.
- C. Three.
- D. Ninety.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why neighbours once complained about bees on a roof.
- B. How ninety kilograms of honey is taken in the last week.
- C. How one young woman keeps three bee boxes on a roof.
- D. What her father said about weather and honey.
**Answer** C

## C6-03 灯塔看守人的最后一个冬天
**Passage**
Shen Hai has kept the light at Cape Yi for thirty-one years, and this winter is his last. The light is automated now, and the relay room runs on a timer, so a keeper is only needed to check the lens, cut the weeds off the path and keep a written record. His record this year has four lines a day: the hour the lamp was checked, the weather, the number of birds on the rocks, and the boats. In the last ten years he has written about fourteen thousand lines. He keeps the oldest book in a drawer and the newest one on the desk, because a book that is used is hard to read. Three boats a week still call in, mostly fishing boats, and he writes down each name. Two winters ago he began writing a fourth kind of line, the name of the student who came up with him, and last winter four students did. In January the fog came for eleven days and the record for those days says the same thing eleven times. He will not say whether that is the job he liked or the job he did not. What he says is that the sea does not care who writes it down, and that somebody has to write it.
**Q1**
How many lines does Shen Hai write in his record each day?
- A. Four.
- B. Three.
- C. Eleven.
- D. Six.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
Why does he keep the oldest book in a drawer and the newest one on the desk?
- A. Because the drawer is dry and the desk is not.
- B. Because he wants to finish the newest one first.
- C. Because a book that is used is hard to read.
- D. Because the office asks him to keep only one book.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
About how many lines has he written in the last ten years?
- A. About four thousand.
- B. About fourteen thousand.
- C. About eleven hundred.
- D. About thirty-one thousand.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
How did the number of the new kind of line change?
- A. There were eleven new lines last winter.
- B. There were four new lines this winter and two last winter.
- C. There were two new lines last winter and none this winter.
- D. There were two lines two winters ago and four last winter.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What does Shen Hai mean by saying that the sea does not care who writes it down?
- A. Somebody has to write it down, whoever that person is.
- B. Nobody reads the record, so writing it is a waste of time.
- C. The boats should write their own names in the book.
- D. The lamp would work just as well without a keeper.
**Answer** A

## C6-04 退休教师的三层花架
**Passage**
Ms. Luo taught history for thirty-two years and retired in March. She lives on the sixth floor, and in April she built a shelf of three boards against the balcony rail. The bottom board is for things that can survive being forgotten: two pots of green onion and a bag of soil. The middle board is for her three pots of tomato, and she counts them. The top board is for the cuttings she keeps because a friend gave them to her. She waters at seven, before the sun turns the balcony into a hot plate, and the whole job takes fifteen minutes. Watering at five in the evening would be better for the tomatoes and worse for her back. In her first summer she grew nine tomatoes and gave away seven. In her second she grew five and gave away five, which she says is a better year. She has a note book in which each pot has a page, and on the page she writes only two things: the date it was planted and whether it fruited. She stopped writing about watering in June of the first year, because she had been doing the same thing every morning for a hundred days and had nothing new to say.
**Q1**
What does Ms. Luo put on the bottom board?
- A. Her three pots of tomato.
- B. The cuttings that a friend gave her.
- C. Nothing at all there that needs daily care.
- D. Two green-onion pots and a bag of soil.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
Why does she water at seven in the morning rather than at five in the evening?
- A. Because the office building is closed in the evening.
- B. Because evening water suits the tomatoes but not her back.
- C. Because the tomato leaves burn in the evening sun.
- D. Because her friend waters the balcony for her in the evening.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
Why did she stop writing about watering in June of the first year?
- A. Because her note book filled up that June.
- B. Because she gave the note book back to her friend.
- C. Because she did the same thing for a hundred days.
- D. Because the green onion in the bottom board died in June.
**Answer** C
**Q4**
In which summer did she give away all the tomatoes she grew?
- A. In the second summer.
- B. In the first summer.
- C. In both summers.
- D. In neither summer.
**Answer** A
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why tomatoes are hard to grow on a sixth-floor balcony.
- B. What a retired history teacher does with a gift from a friend.
- C. How many tomatoes one family can grow in a summer.
- D. How a retired teacher keeps three boards going on a balcony.
**Answer** D

## C6-05 只开三个月的渡船
**Passage**
The ferry at Shuangzhou runs from the first of May to the last of July and not one day more. The skipper is a man of fifty-eight called Lu Ming, and he has made the crossing eleven thousand times in twenty-two years. In the other nine months the boat lies at the north bank under a cover of blue tarpaulin, and Lu Ming works on engines at a garage in the county town. The crossing takes thirty-five minutes each way and the fare is fifteen yuan. In those three months the ferry carries about four thousand people, which is a good year; a bad one is two thousand. Lu Ming keeps two lists, one for the passengers and one for the freight, and he writes the freight list in pencil because the paper gets wet. His daughter is an accountant in the city and has offered to put the numbers into a computer for him. He has not said yes. When the ferry runs, he writes the time he left and the time he arrived, and at the end of July he turns the page and starts the next year from one again.
**Q1**
How long does the ferry run each year?
- A. All the year round without a single break.
- B. From the first of May to July.
- C. From the first of May to September.
- D. For about eleven months of every year.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
What does Lu Ming do during the other nine months?
- A. He works on engines at a garage in the county town.
- B. He takes passengers over in a small rowing boat.
- C. He writes lists for the county town office.
- D. He stays on the north bank beside the boat under a cover.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
What can we learn about the number of passengers?
- A. It is always about the same in every year.
- B. It was about two thousand people in that very last good year.
- C. A bad year is always about four thousand.
- D. A good year is about four thousand, a bad one two thousand.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
Why does Lu Ming write the freight list in pencil?
- A. Because he has no other pencil.
- B. Because pencil can be bought in the county town.
- C. Because the paper gets wet.
- D. Because his daughter prefers pencil.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What does the story of the two lists tell us about Lu Ming?
- A. That he does not trust his daughter.
- B. That he keeps his own plain records by hand.
- C. That the county town asked him to count the freight.
- D. That the ferry carried four thousand people last May.
**Answer** B

## C6-06 裁缝口袋里的小尺子
**Passage**
Mr. Liang has a tape measure in his pocket that is not a tape measure. It is a small steel ruler about fifteen centimetres long with a hole in one end, and he has carried it since he opened his shop. The shop takes in clothes that other shops will not touch: coats with strange linings, curtains with a metre of extra cloth sewn into the hem, trousers that belonged to a great-grandfather. People say he looks at a garment for a long time before he says a price, and they are right, but what he is doing is not thinking. He is finding the seam. Once the original seam is visible, the alteration is easy, because the cloth tells you where it was meant to end. He has never thrown away a garment that he could not mend, and he has sent back four to their owners rather than cut them up. His shop opens at nine and shuts at six, and between one and two he eats at the same table as his two apprentices. The ruler cost him nine yuan in 1979 and he says it is the only tool he owns that has never needed to be replaced.
**Q1**
What is unusual about the ruler in Mr. Liang's pocket?
- A. It cannot measure anything longer than fifteen centimetres.
- B. It was made in 1979 and is therefore very old.
- C. It is a small steel ruler, not a tape measure.
- D. He keeps it only for measuring curtains at the shop.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
What does Mr. Liang do before he tells a customer the price?
- A. He measures the garment twice.
- B. He asks the customer what she paid for it.
- C. He checks whether the shop can finish the work.
- D. He finds the original seam.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
Why is the original seam so important to him?
- A. Because it shows how much the garment cost him.
- B. Because once the seam is visible the alteration is easy.
- C. Because every garment that comes into his shop has a visible seam.
- D. Because his father taught him how to find it.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
Why did he send four garments back instead of cutting them up?
- A. Because he never throws away a mendable garment.
- B. Because his two apprentices refused to do that kind of work.
- C. Because the shop was closed for four days that week.
- D. Because the customers came back for them after four days.
**Answer** A
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why a tailor keeps an old steel ruler.
- B. How curtains are measured before they are cut.
- C. How one tailor decides what work he can and cannot take.
- D. What a shop that opens at nine and shuts at six is.
**Answer** C
