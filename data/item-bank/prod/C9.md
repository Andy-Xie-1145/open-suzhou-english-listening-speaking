## C9-01 冬天推车卖烤红薯
**Passage**
Uncle Yan pushes a cart of roast sweet potatoes into the market square daily from the tenth of November to the tenth of February, and no day before or after. He buys them eighty kilometres away at four yuan a kilo and sells them at six, so a good day is about thirty kilos and a bad one fifteen. He says the bad ones are the warm days, when people still want ice, not something hot. The oven under the cart holds six kilos at a time and takes fifty minutes, so he can turn over a batch every hour from three in the afternoon. The square is closed to carts on Sunday morning, and that is when he washes the cart, which costs him an hour he does not lose, since he is not there anyway. He keeps the burnt ones for himself and gives them to the man who moves the bins; he does not sell them at a lower price. He has pushed the same cart for nineteen years and has never once been late, not even when snow closed the road in the first week. He says what has really cost him is his left knee, and he blames the slope at the north end of the square rather than the cold.
**Q1**
How long does Uncle Yan sell sweet potatoes in the square?
- A. All year round.
- B. From the tenth of February to the tenth of November.
- C. From the tenth of November to the tenth of March.
- D. From the tenth of November to the tenth of February.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
What makes a day bad for him?
- A. When the price of sweet potatoes rises in the far town.
- B. When the weather is warm and people still want ice.
- C. When the square is closed to carts.
- D. When the man who moves the bins does not come.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
How much can the oven under the cart hold at one time?
- A. Two kilos.
- B. Four kilos.
- C. Six kilos.
- D. Twenty kilos.
**Answer** C
**Q4**
Why does he wash the cart on Sunday morning?
- A. Because the square is closed to carts then.
- B. Because the sweet potatoes are sold faster on Sunday.
- C. Because the oven is cleaner on Sunday.
- D. Because the man who moves the bins comes then.
**Answer** A
**Q5**
What does he blame for his left knee?
- A. The snow that closed the road in the first week of that year.
- B. The slope at the north end of the square rather than the cold.
- C. Nineteen years of pushing the same cart every week.
- D. The oven under the cart, which takes fifty minutes for each batch.
**Answer** B

## C9-02 教盲人用手机的志愿者
**Passage**
Mr. Pei is seventy-eight and teaches four people to use a mobile phone, and he learned all of it after he lost his sight. That is the whole of his qualification, and he says it out loud before anybody asks. The four are all over sixty and none of them wanted to be taught by their own children. The lesson is ninety minutes and it never changes: find the phone, find the volume button, ring one number, stop. Anything else is a second lesson, and there have been eleven second lessons. He keeps the phones in a box with the numbers written on the outside in large print, because the phones themselves are all different. His own phone has no case, since he cannot feel a case anyway. He says the hardest part is not the buttons; it is that a blind person cannot see a stranger's face turn away when they cannot help. In the fourth year one of his four wrote a number into his hand at the end of the lesson, and Mr. Pei asked whether it was a telephone or a taxi, and both of them laughed for a while.
**Q1**
Why does Mr. Pei say he is the right person to teach this?
- A. Because he lost his sight and had to learn to use a phone.
- B. Because he worked in a telephone office for forty years before all this.
- C. Because he has read about phones in a newspaper.
- D. Because his own children use phones every day.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
What happens in a second lesson?
- A. He teaches the same four steps again.
- B. The students bring a relative with them.
- C. Something is taught that is not one of the four steps.
- D. He explains what a phone is for, before the lesson starts.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
Why does he keep the phones in a box with large numbers on the outside?
- A. Because the box keeps all the phones safe and sound.
- B. Because the students cannot feel the numbers on them.
- C. Because the phones have different buttons on them, in different places on every single one.
- D. Because the phones are all different and the students cannot tell them apart by touch.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
Why does he say the hardest part is not the buttons?
- A. Because the buttons are in a language he cannot read.
- B. Because a blind person cannot see a stranger's face turn away.
- C. Because the phone is too heavy to hold for a long time.
- D. Because his students forget the four steps.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What does the passage tell us about the four people he teaches?
- A. They are all over sixty and none of them wanted to be taught by their own children.
- B. They all work in the same office.
- C. They have each paid him for the lesson.
- D. They keep their old phones in a drawer at home, with the numbers written outside it.
**Answer** A

## C9-03 每年只画一幅画的老人
**Passage**
Mr. Deng painted one picture a year for thirty-one years, and he has kept every one of them. They are small, about the size of a sheet of paper, and they are all of the same street seen from the same window. One a year was not a rule he set; it was what the calendar allowed, because he paints in the first week of January when the light is flat. In 2009 he painted nothing at all, and the gap is the only one. He says the year without a picture was the year he stopped speaking to his brother, and that the painting had nothing to do with it, though he can understand why people think it does. When a student from the college asked to see the pictures, he showed her four, and then eleven, and then stopped counting. Three of them have been to exhibitions. The rest are in a drawer under the window in no order at all. He is seventy-six now and says he is not sure there will be a thirty-second, because his hands have begun to shake in the afternoon and the first week of January is in the afternoon.
**Q1**
How big are the pictures?
- A. About the size of a tall wooden door.
- B. About the size of a postcard.
- C. About the size of a sheet of paper.
- D. About the size of a shop window.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
When does he paint?
- A. In the first week of January.
- B. In the last week of December.
- C. On Sunday afternoons in summer.
- D. Whenever the weather is fine.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
What does the passage say about 2009?
- A. Because his hands began to shake that year.
- B. Because it was the year he stopped speaking to his brother.
- C. Because the college asked to see all the pictures that year.
- D. Because the light was not flat that year.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
How did he answer the student from the college?
- A. He showed her all thirty-one pictures.
- B. He showed her none of them, because they are his own.
- C. He gave her the three that had been to exhibitions.
- D. He showed her four, then eleven, and then stopped counting.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why his hands have begun to shake in the afternoon light in recent years.
- B. How three of his pictures came to be shown at exhibitions.
- C. How one man has painted the same street once a year for thirty-one years.
- D. Why he stopped speaking to his brother in 2009.
**Answer** C

## C9-04 社区菜园里的三分地
**Passage**
The community garden has forty-one plots of three metres by two. Auntie Fu has plot number nineteen, and she counts everything, because two plots near her were taken by people who never came and one of those is now full of weeds nobody will clear. Her own plot has never had a weed in it. She grows greens, beans, two rows of spring onions and one squash, and the squash is the only thing she does not eat herself; it goes to the table in the yard on the last Sunday of the month. Watering is by a rota of eight people, three in the morning and one in the evening, in summer. Auntie Fu is always in the evening group and has never once been late, and the rota is on a board by the tap. The gate is shut at six and opened again at seven in the morning, and the key hangs on the second nail. Two years ago she offered her plot to a family from the next street and the family never came; she kept it and has never offered again. She says three metres by two is enough for four people if nobody plants potatoes.
**Q1**
How big is Auntie Fu's plot?
- A. Two metres by two.
- B. Three metres by two.
- C. Four metres by two.
- D. Three metres by three.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
How is the garden watered in summer?
- A. Each plot holder waters only her own plot.
- B. A paid man waters the whole garden once a day.
- C. Three people come in the morning and one stays there all the afternoon.
- D. Three people come in the morning and one in the evening, in turn.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
What happens to a plot whose holder never comes?
- A. It can end up full of weeds nobody will clear.
- B. The council gives it to a family from the next street.
- C. Auntie Fu takes it back at once.
- D. It is ploughed under in the winter.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What does she do with the one squash?
- A. She eats it herself like everything else.
- B. She gives it to the family from the next street, as she once offered.
- C. She puts it on the table in the yard on the last Sunday.
- D. She keeps it for her own family.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
Why does she say three metres by two is enough for four people?
- A. Because the garden has forty-one plots in it all.
- B. Because that is the size of her own plot, where four kinds of vegetable grow.
- C. Because the key to the gate hangs on the second nail, right up above it.
- D. Because the rota has eight names on it.
**Answer** B

## C9-05 在阳台上养蚕的男生
**Passage**
Yu Tian keeps silkworms on a balcony on the eleventh floor, and last spring he took four hundred of them out of a paper bag his uncle had brought from the country. A tray is ten boxes, a box holds about thirty, and a tray therefore feeds about three hundred. He started with two trays, which was one too many, and by the last week he had three trays and half a bag of leaves left over. He feeds them at seven in the morning and at seven in the evening, and he says the evening feed is the one they wait for. The cocoons are made on a frame of dry twigs and are ready after about nine days. He keeps the best twenty for the moth case and gives the rest to the school, which will not feed them but will let the children look. In the autumn the moths come out, lay on paper, and die within three days. He has kept every paper. Nothing in the flat is used for anything else now.
**Q1**
About how many silkworms does one tray feed, at about thirty to a box and ten boxes to a tray?
- A. About three hundred.
- B. About thirty.
- C. About ten.
- D. About four hundred.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
How long do the cocoons take to become ready?
- A. About three days.
- B. About seven days.
- C. About nine days.
- D. About eleven days.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
Why does he keep the best twenty cocoons?
- A. To give them to the school.
- B. For the moth case.
- C. To sell them in the autumn.
- D. To feed them through the winter.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
What happens to the moths in the autumn?
- A. They live for three weeks and then fly away.
- B. They are kept alive through the winter on the balcony.
- C. They are taken to the school to be looked at.
- D. They lay on paper and die within three days.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What does the passage tell us about Yu Tian's flat?
- A. Nothing in it is used for anything else now.
- B. It has two balconies and he uses the larger one.
- C. His uncle comes from the country every spring.
- D. He keeps the trays on the balcony in the winter as well.
**Answer** A

## C9-06 在候车亭画画的女孩
**Passage**
Ke Yan paints at the bus shelter at the end of the road on Saturday and Sunday afternoons, and the shelter is nine metres long, which gives her room for a stool, a box of water and a board. She is nineteen and has been doing it for three years. She does the same view: the road, the wall behind it and the two poles. What changes is the weather, and she has done that view in rain, in fog and in a heat that made the paint dry on the board before she could use it. She sells nothing and takes a hat, not a box for money. People stop; about eight of them say something and the rest look and go, and she has never once found that rude. The wall behind the view is scheduled to be painted over in the spring, and the council has not decided what colour. She says she will do the new wall too, and that the view will be the same, and that this is not a reason to stop. Her teacher thinks she should go to art college. She has not decided and she has until the spring.
**Q1**
How long is the bus shelter?
- A. About six metres.
- B. About twelve metres.
- C. About nine metres.
- D. About three metres.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
What changes in her picture from week to week?
- A. The road, the wall and the two poles.
- B. The size of the board.
- C. The people who stop at the shelter.
- D. The weather.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
What does she take to the shelter?
- A. A hat, not a box for money.
- B. A box for money and a hat.
- C. A stool and a large box.
- D. An easel and a parasol.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What has she found about the people who stop?
- A. That about eight of them ask her to stop.
- B. That about eight say something and the rest look and go.
- C. That most of them ask her for money.
- D. That they never look at the picture at all, ever again.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
Why does she say the spring is not a reason to stop?
- A. Because the council has already chosen the new colour for the wall.
- B. Because her teacher wants her to keep painting.
- C. Because the two poles will be taken away.
- D. Because she will paint the new wall and keep the same view.
**Answer** D
