## C7-01 每周三晚上开机的短波电台
**Passage**
Han Yu is fourteen and owns a radio that most people in his street cannot switch on. It stood in a cupboard in his grandfather's house for eleven years before he asked to have it. The first thing he learned was that a radio has to be warmed up; the dial only means something after the set has been on for about ten minutes. He now listens on Wednesday evenings, because that is when the station two thousand kilometres away sends its reading at nine. He writes the numbers in a school exercise book, with the date at the top of the page, and by the end of a year the book is full. His grandfather kept the same numbers for eleven years without writing them down, and Han Yu has found four of his pages in a biscuit tin, each with a different date on it. The station is not on every Wednesday; it misses two or three a year, and Han Yu has learned to tell the difference between a station that is off and a set that is broken, because a broken set hums and an off station is silent. He has never told his class why he does this.
**Q1**
What is the first thing Han Yu learned about his radio?
- A. The dial only means something after the set has been on for about ten minutes.
- B. The set has to be carried outside before it will ever really work at all.
- C. The station transmits only on Wednesday evenings.
- D. The dial has to be cleaned every week.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
How long had the radio been in the cupboard?
- A. Eleven weeks.
- B. Four years.
- C. Eleven years.
- D. Two years.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
Why does Han Yu listen on Wednesday evenings?
- A. Because his grandfather told him to.
- B. Because the station two thousand kilometres away sends its reading at nine that evening.
- C. Because the school exercise book is only really free on Wednesday at all then.
- D. Because his class meets on Wednesday.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
How can Han Yu tell that the set is broken rather than the station off?
- A. The station sends a short notice before it ever stops.
- B. His grandfather's book has four pages in it.
- C. The set becomes warmer than usual.
- D. A broken set hums and an off station is silent.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How a boy keeps a written record of a radio station that his grandfather also kept.
- B. Why most people in his street cannot use a radio.
- C. What really happens to a radio that is just left alone in a cupboard for years.
- D. How a school exercise book gets full in one year.
**Answer** A

## C7-02 修了四十年自行车的老师傅
**Passage**
Mr. Dai has repaired bicycles for forty-one years from a stall the size of a small table, and he can name the year of a bicycle from the shape of its chain. He says a bicycle tells you its age before you count anything: the chain gets thin at the pins, the brake blocks get small, and the pedal ends go flat. He charges eight yuan for a full service, which takes about an hour, and he does not charge extra for a bicycle that is old. In his first ten years he fixed about four hundred bicycles a year. Now it is close to a hundred and twenty, and he says that is not because people ride less, but because they ride further. The nearest proper shop is four kilometres away, and a bus to it costs two yuan each way. A boy brought in a bicycle last month and asked whether it was worth repairing. Mr. Dai turned the back wheel, listened to it, and said that the frame was sound and one wheel needed a new spoke; that is forty yuan and twenty minutes, and the boy will keep it for years. The stall has no sign, and Mr. Dai has never put one up.
**Q1**
How does Mr. Dai decide how old a bicycle is?
- A. He counts the scratches on the frame.
- B. He just asks the owner how long it has really been used.
- C. He reads the year written on the bell.
- D. He looks at the chain, the brake blocks and the pedal ends.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
How much does Mr. Dai charge for a full service?
- A. Twenty yuan.
- B. Eight yuan.
- C. Forty yuan.
- D. One hundred and twenty yuan.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
Why does he say the number of bicycles he repairs has fallen?
- A. People ride further than they used to, and the nearest proper shop is four kilometres away.
- B. People ride less than they used to.
- C. His stall has really no sign at all, so new customers never even really find him.
- D. Eight yuan is more than it was forty-one years ago.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What did Mr. Dai tell the boy about his bicycle last month?
- A. That the frame was not worth repairing.
- B. That the whole bicycle needed a new chain.
- C. That the frame was sound but one wheel needed a new spoke.
- D. That it would really be much cheaper to buy a new bicycle.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why a bicycle gets more expensive as it gets older.
- B. What a man who has repaired bicycles for forty-one years looks at, and what he charges.
- C. How far it really is from that stall to the nearest proper shop at all then.
- D. Why the stall has never had a sign.
**Answer** B

## C7-03 只卖三种面包的小店
**Passage**
The bakery on Green Street sells three kinds of bread and has done for nineteen years. The owner, Auntie Mo, will not add a fourth, and she has been asked about it so often that she keeps a list of the ones that were refused. They are a cheese loaf, a cake, and a roll with olives in it. Her bread is made once a day, at two in the morning, and there are ninety loaves by six. If any are left at nine they go to the door of the primary school, and if they are still there at eleven they go home with the staff. Nothing is sold on the second day, which surprises people. Green, her assistant, is twenty-three and has worked there for four years. She can tell a customer who has bought the plain loaf every week for two years by the way they look at the shelf first, before they look at her. Auntie Mo says the shop has never sold a thing that was not baked in that building, and that this is the only rule she has. In the second year the queue for the red bean bun reached the corner, and Auntie Mo did not make more than ninety that day either.
**Q1**
What does Auntie Mo do with bread that is left at nine?
- A. She keeps it until the next morning.
- B. She sells it at a lower price in the afternoon.
- C. She takes it to the door of the primary school.
- D. She gives it to the staff at eleven.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
Why is the bread made at two in the morning?
- A. So that ninety loaves are ready by six.
- B. So that the oven is cold enough at midnight.
- C. So that the staff arrive at six.
- D. So that school children get it at nine.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
How can Green tell a customer who has bought the plain loaf every week for two years?
- A. By the money that the customer ever puts on the shelf at all.
- B. By the name the customer calls Auntie Mo.
- C. By the hour the customer comes in.
- D. By the way the customer looks at the shelf before looking at her.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
According to Auntie Mo, what is the only rule the shop has?
- A. Nobody at all may ever buy any bread in the shop after nine.
- B. The shop never sells a thing that was not baked in that building.
- C. Bread that is left over is never sold at a lower price.
- D. There are always ninety loaves a day.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What does the passage tell us about the red bean bun?
- A. It is the kind Auntie Mo refuses to bake, and her bread is made at two in the morning.
- B. It is the kind that takes longest to make, and her bread is made at two in the morning.
- C. Auntie Mo has written it on her refused list, and her bread is made at two in the morning.
- D. It is the kind that brings a queue, but she still did not bake more than ninety that day.
**Answer** D

## C7-04 教孩子游泳的老教练
**Passage**
Coach Xie taught at the town pool for thirty-six years and never learned to swim well himself, because a coach in that pool only ever taught the shallow end. He knows nine children who can float at three, and he can tell from the way they stand at the edge which ones will. The rule in his pool is that a child is never held by the arms; a child is held under the chest, so that the arms stay free. He taught four hundred and sixty children in his first ten years and a hundred and thirty in his last ten. The reason is not that the pool closed, because the pool was open all ten years, but that more of the children came with an adult on each side. He still keeps the paper list from his first year. Forty names on it are written in a hand that is not his, because the first year he wrote down the names of children who could not yet swim and one of the mothers filled them in. He says a list is only useful if you can still read it.
**Q1**
Why did Coach Xie never learn to swim well himself?
- A. Because the pool there was really too shallow just near the door.
- B. Because a coach in that pool only ever taught the shallow end.
- C. Because he was afraid of the deep water.
- D. Because he never went to the pool himself.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
How is a child held in the water in his pool?
- A. By the arms.
- B. By just one arm only, at the shallow end.
- C. By the legs.
- D. Under the chest, so that the arms stay free.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
Why does he teach far fewer children now than in his first ten years?
- A. Because the pool closed for ten years.
- B. Because there are really far fewer children left in the town these days.
- C. Because more of the children now come with an adult on each side.
- D. Because he stopped keeping the paper list.
**Answer** C
**Q4**
Why are forty names on his first-year list not in his handwriting?
- A. Because the mothers of children who could not yet swim filled them in.
- B. Because a student from his own class wrote them all down for him.
- C. Because the list was bought second-hand.
- D. Because he wrote the list out twice.
**Answer** A
**Q5**
What does he mean by saying that a list is only useful if you can still read it?
- A. He thinks the old list should be thrown away.
- B. He means the old paper from that first year has really all faded away.
- C. He means the names are worth keeping only while they can still be used.
- D. He means the list is now too long to keep.
**Answer** C

## C7-05 用废木头做玩具车的老人
**Passage**
Uncle Bai began making toy cars out of wood boxes in the spring of last year, when a shop on his street gave away its packing cases. He uses the flat sides, cuts a wheel from the corner where the wood is strongest, and nails four on. A car takes him about forty minutes and holds three children on its floor, which he thinks is enough. He made seventy-four last year and gave sixty-one away. The other thirteen are on a shelf in the community room, and a child may take one and leave it there for the next one. He keeps the list of who brought the box and what the car is worth to him, which is nothing, though it cost him about six yuan in nails. His rule is that a child must be able to reach the floor, so the height is fixed and he will not change it for anybody. Last winter he made eighteen in a month when he was not well, and he says that he would not do it again, and that he has already started eleven this month.
**Q1**
Where does Uncle Bai get the wood for the cars?
- A. From packing cases that a shop gives away.
- B. From a timber yard just near the market.
- C. From the shelf in the community room.
- D. From his own house.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
About how long does one toy car take him?
- A. Six minutes.
- B. Forty minutes.
- C. Seventy-four minutes.
- D. Thirteen minutes.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
How many cars did he give away last year?
- A. Thirteen.
- B. Seventy-four.
- C. Fourteen.
- D. Sixty-one.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
Why does he fix the height of the car and refuse to change it?
- A. Because the nails are too short for a taller car.
- B. Because a taller car needs more wood.
- C. Because a child must be able to reach the floor.
- D. Because the shop does not give away large cases.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How one old man makes toy cars out of thrown-away packing cases.
- B. Why the community room keeps a shelf of toy cars.
- C. What Uncle Bai does in the winter when he is not well.
- D. How much wood one toy car needs.
**Answer** A

## C7-06 一天走四十里的送货员
**Passage**
Old Chen delivers bread and cakes to forty-two shops in the town, and he does it on foot because there is no room in the van for a bicycle as well. He starts at three in the morning at the bakery and finishes by ten. The round is about twenty kilometres, and on a hot day he has to stop three times. He carries two bags, one for cakes and one for bread, and the cakes go in first because they are light and nothing can be put under them in the other bag. He has done this for twenty-six years and knows which shops will be open and which will still be shut at four. He does not knock at the back door of any shop; he leaves the bag in the gap by the door and writes the time on the top of it. If he finds the bag still there on the way back, he knows it was not taken. Last month he wrote a new time on the same bag six times in one morning because the door of that shop was blocked by a delivery of paint, and he says that he never complains about it, because a shop that cannot open is not his business.
**Q1**
Why does Old Chen deliver on foot?
- A. Because the van is really used for other work in the early morning.
- B. Because the town is too small for a van.
- C. Because there is no room in the van for a bicycle as well.
- D. Because all the shops are within walking distance of one another.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
Why do the cakes go in the first bag?
- A. Because the first bag is the larger one, and he carries two bags on foot.
- B. Because the shops sell more bread than cakes, and he carries two bags on foot.
- C. Because the bread is delivered at four, and he does the whole round on foot.
- D. Because they are light and nothing can be put under them in the other bag.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
How can Old Chen tell that a shop has taken a bag?
- A. Because the bag is not there when he comes back.
- B. Because the shopkeeper thanks him at the door.
- C. Because he sees the shopkeeper through the glass.
- D. Because the time he wrote on it is really different.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What happened outside the shop whose door was blocked by paint?
- A. Old Chen really left the bag in the gap by the door as usual.
- B. He wrote a new time on the same bag six times in one morning.
- C. He walked the whole round a second time.
- D. He complained to the shopkeeper about the paint.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What does the passage tell us about the last shop in his round?
- A. That it is really the only shop in the town that he does not deliver to.
- B. That it is the shop he complains about most.
- C. That it is the nearest one to the bakery.
- D. That its being unable to open early is a matter for the shop, not for him.
**Answer** D
