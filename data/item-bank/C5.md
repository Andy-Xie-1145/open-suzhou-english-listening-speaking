## C5-01 地下室里的合唱团
**Passage**
Guan Laoshi taught maths for thirty-four years, and when he retired nobody expected him to become a singer. Two winters ago the women in his building asked whether the empty room on the ground floor could be used on Wednesday evenings, and he said yes. He expected five people to come. Twenty-six came. They meet from seven to eight thirty, so every rehearsal lasts ninety minutes, and they begin by singing the same slow song twice, because a choir cannot listen to itself while it is hurrying. Most members are women over fifty, but four are men, and two of them are still in their twenties. Auntie Lin is seventy-three and cannot read music at all. She keeps the second row on the left and learns every song by ear. The piano in that room is tuned twice a year. Mr. Guan says a choir of twenty-six does not need a full hall; what it needs is a quiet room. Last winter the choir gave its first concert, and in the first hour only thirty people came in. Mr. Guan was not disappointed about that either. The concert was held in the community hall. The members will meet again this Wednesday, as they have every week for two winters.
**Q1**
How long does each rehearsal last?
- A. Thirty minutes.
- B. Ninety minutes.
- C. Sixty minutes.
- D. One hundred and twenty minutes.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
How does Auntie Lin learn the songs?
- A. She reads them from a printed song book.
- B. She watches the hands of the piano teacher.
- C. She listens to them and learns them by heart.
- D. She practises with the piano before every rehearsal.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
How many more people came to the first rehearsal than Mr. Guan had expected?
- A. Twenty-one.
- B. Twenty-six.
- C. Thirty-one.
- D. Five.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What can we learn about the choir from the way Mr. Guan works with them every Wednesday?
- A. The members prefer short songs to long ones.
- B. Mr. Guan wants the members to learn new songs very quickly.
- C. The choir puts more value on careful listening than on a large audience.
- D. The members sing the same song twice because they have poor memories.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why a retired maths teacher began to sing alone.
- B. How a small neighbourhood choir was started and kept going.
- C. Why the first concert drew only thirty people.
- D. How Wednesday evenings changed one street.
**Answer** B

## C5-02 博物馆的少年讲解员
**Passage**
Zhou Yang is fifteen and had never spoken to a group before. Last spring he walked into the small history museum because it was raining and he had nowhere to wait. A guide asked whether he would like to help on Saturday mornings, and he said yes. His first job was to carry a box of old photographs between two rooms, while the guide taught him the names. The main room has forty objects, and Zhou Yang can answer most questions about them. Two he still cannot: a brown cup and a wooden door handle. He keeps them on a card in his pocket. Visitors are not always easy. Last month a man asked him three times whether the ship in the big picture had really been built in his town. Zhou Yang does not know, so he said he would find out and tell the visitor the next day. He did find out, and the man came back on Sunday and waited. Zhou Yang says 'I don't know' at once when he has to. The guide calls it the most useful sentence a young guide can learn. The card in his pocket now has a third line on it: the date of the ship painting, which he looked up himself.
**Q1**
Why did Zhou Yang first come to the museum?
- A. He wanted to learn the history of his town.
- B. His teacher sent him there to finish a project.
- C. It was raining and he had nowhere to wait.
- D. He was looking for his aunt who worked there.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
What was Zhou Yang's first job in the museum?
- A. Carrying a box of old photographs between two rooms.
- B. Cleaning the glass cases in the main room.
- C. Answering visitors' questions about the old ship.
- D. Showing visitors the two objects he could not name.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
Of the forty objects in the main room, how many can Zhou Yang usually answer questions about?
- A. Two.
- B. Forty.
- C. Twenty.
- D. Thirty-eight.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
What is the card in Zhou Yang's pocket for?
- A. To remember the names of all forty objects in the main room every day.
- B. To write down what he cannot answer, and to add what he finds out later.
- C. To show the guide how well he answers visitors' questions on Saturdays.
- D. To keep a record of the visitors who come to the museum on Saturdays.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How a shy boy became a museum guide people could trust.
- B. How a small museum keeps forty old objects.
- C. Why one visitor asked about the ship three times.
- D. How a guide taught a boy to use a small card.
**Answer** A

## C5-03 清晨五点的蔬菜摊
**Passage**
Chen Shun's mother has sold vegetables at the same corner for nineteen years. She gets up at five, and by half past six the workers from the breakfast factory are already queueing, so Chen Shun used to think the job was easy. Last summer he offered to help, and his mother let him keep the money for one week. He wrote every number on the back of an old calendar. On the first day he took eleven hundred and forty yuan and was proud of it. On the seventh day he wrote the same number again, and his mother asked him a question he could not answer: the stall on the next corner had taken nine hundred yuan more than he had on Monday. She wanted him to find out why. It was not the price. Chen Shun had given away three bags of spinach because the man looked tired, and had thrown away half a box of beans at closing time without counting them. He says he still cannot answer all his mother's questions, and that this is fine, because her questions turn on numbers. Now he writes the numbers down before he gives anything away, and he counts the boxes before he empties them. On Sunday mornings he still goes to the corner with her.
**Q1**
What time does Chen Shun's mother start work at the market?
- A. At half past six.
- B. At five.
- C. At six.
- D. At half past five.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
How did Chen Shun lose money during that week?
- A. He sold his vegetables at too low a price.
- B. He gave his old calendar to the stall next door.
- C. He wrote the numbers down after he gave things away.
- D. He gave away spinach and threw away beans.
**Answer** D
**Q3**
Why did Chen Shun's mother ask about the stall on the next corner instead of about his prices?
- A. She wanted him to see that his own losses were the reason.
- B. She thought the price of beans was too low.
- C. She was angry that he had sold so little.
- D. She wanted him to close the stall on the next corner.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What does Chen Shun mean by saying he still cannot answer all his mother's questions, and that this is fine?
- A. His mother asks him far too many questions.
- B. He plans to close the stall next summer.
- C. He has learnt that writing the numbers down is what helps.
- D. He wants to become a shopkeeper rather than a seller.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why vegetable prices rise before a holiday.
- B. How the workers of a breakfast factory queue for vegetables.
- C. What one market corner looks like after nineteen years.
- D. How a boy learnt to watch the money in his mother's stall.
**Answer** D

## C5-04 少了一天的暑期课
**Passage**
Lin Hao and Zhao Min went to a village summer school where the term was fifteen teaching days long. They taught English and art, and they had prepared forty picture cards for the first lesson. On the second morning the head teacher told them that the next day would have no lessons at all, because he had to take his mother to hospital in the county town. Lin Hao wanted to start anyway, but Zhao Min refused. She said that thirty-one children cannot learn from two strangers, so they spent that free day walking up and down the village road and learning every child's name. One whole day out of the fifteen was gone. They still finished the course, because they never gave the children a full morning of copying: every morning was a new lesson, and every afternoon the children played with the cards on the ground. On the last morning the head teacher asked them to leave the forty cards in the village. Lin Hao says they are still on a shelf there, and that is enough. The village school opens again in three years, and the two students have not decided yet whether they will go back.
**Q1**
How many picture cards did Lin Hao and Zhao Min prepare?
- A. Forty.
- B. Thirty-one.
- C. Fifteen.
- D. Fourteen.
**Answer** A
**Q2**
Why was there no lesson on that day?
- A. The village school was closed for a festival.
- B. The children were away working with their parents.
- C. The head teacher had to take his mother to hospital.
- D. The road to the county town was closed by snow.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
How many teaching days of the course did the children actually get?
- A. Fifteen.
- B. Fourteen.
- C. Thirty-one.
- D. Thirteen.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
What did losing that teaching day cost, and what did that free day show the two students about what the children needed?
- A. One of the fifteen teaching days, and that the children learn best from a quiet morning of copying.
- B. One of the fifteen teaching days, and that the children already knew the two students well enough to learn from them.
- C. One of the fifteen teaching days, and that the children needed the forty picture cards more than any new lesson.
- D. One of the fifteen teaching days, and that the children needed new lessons and names rather than a quiet morning of copying.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How two students dealt with a lost day in a village course.
- B. Why village children prefer playing with cards to copying.
- C. How a head teacher looks after his old mother.
- D. What fifteen teaching days of English usually contain.
**Answer** A

## C5-05 红灯笼电影院关门以后
**Passage**
The last film in the Red Lantern Cinema was shown nine years ago, and Zhou Deming still walks past it every Tuesday. He is seventy-nine now. He started in that hall at sixteen as the man who ran the projector, and he stopped at seventy, so he worked in the same room for fifty-four years. In his first winter the hall had a coal stove, and on a Saturday night about four hundred people came. He knew every one of them by the way they sat: the back rows were for the young, and the front for families with small children. Later a new cinema opened across the street with a bigger screen, and the Red Lantern went from four hundred a night to sixty. Zhou Deming did not try to fight that. He says a hall cannot win that race, and that he was paid to show the film correctly, not to keep an audience. Three years ago the building became a warehouse for a shop, and the seats are now under boxes. Zhou Deming has the key, but he keeps it in his pocket. On Tuesdays he walks the whole length of Green Street and home again, at the same hour as the films used to start.
**Q1**
How old was Zhou Deming when the last film was shown in the Red Lantern?
- A. Sixty.
- B. Seventy-nine.
- C. Seventy.
- D. Sixteen.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
About how many people came on a Saturday night in his first winter?
- A. About four hundred.
- B. About sixty.
- C. About one hundred and twenty.
- D. About nine hundred.
**Answer** A
**Q3**
How could Zhou Deming tell his old audience apart?
- A. He knew the names of most of them.
- B. He knew where each family liked to sit.
- C. He asked the ticket seller at the door.
- D. He read the tickets that people handed in.
**Answer** B
**Q4**
Which statement best describes Zhou Deming after the Red Lantern became a warehouse?
- A. He now visits the new cinema every week instead of walking past the old hall.
- B. He is waiting for a chance to open the hall as a cinema again.
- C. He handed the key to the shop that uses the building.
- D. He had already made his peace with the closing, and he still keeps the key to the building.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. Why Saturday nights were busy in the old hall.
- B. How a man watched his cinema close without regret.
- C. How a small street cinema beat a bigger one.
- D. What people in the back rows used to like.
**Answer** B
