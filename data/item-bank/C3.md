## C3-01 会走的水
**Passage**
A tap in the school toilets had been dripping for months, and nobody knew. Last March, four students in Class Three asked to see the school's water meter. They came in early every morning, read the number, and wrote it down. When they compared their first reading with the number their science teacher had copied in January, the gap was far larger than any change they had seen before. One student worked out that a tap dripping once a second loses about thirty litres a day. In three months that is close to two thousand seven hundred litres, enough to fill more than twenty small bathtubs. The four students asked to see the manager, and they walked with him to the toilets. He heard the sound himself, and the old tap was taken out and a new one fitted the next day. The class went on reading the meter for one more month. The number rose by only about the same amount as in an ordinary month, and the students put a short report on the classroom wall. Before winter came, they also checked every other tap in the building.
**Q1**
How often did the students read the water meter last March?
- A. Once a week, in the school hall.
- B. Every morning, before the first class began.
- C. Twice a day, at noon and after school.
- D. Once a month, on the first day of term.
**Answer** B
**Q2**
According to the passage, about how much water does a tap that drips once a second lose in a day?
- A. About three litres.
- B. About one hundred litres.
- C. About thirty litres.
- D. About three hundred litres.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
What did the class do for one more month after the new tap was fitted?
- A. They went on reading the water meter every day.
- B. They waited for winter to check the taps.
- C. They walked to the toilets with the manager.
- D. They counted the cups of water in the school.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What can we learn about the four students?
- A. They hoped to win a prize from the school.
- B. They planned to sell the water that was lost.
- C. They were unhappy with the manager's help.
- D. They made an adult come and see the problem for himself.
**Answer** D
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How students found a waste of water at school.
- B. How a school decides the price of its water.
- C. How to build a small bathroom at home.
- D. How a water meter records every drop of water.
**Answer** A

## C3-02 楼下的红箱子
**Passage**
Every Wednesday an old man walks slowly around the block with two cloth bags. He has done this for six years, and the bags are for the wrong things he takes out of the box. People should not put used batteries, old medicine or broken lamps in the normal rubbish bin. The city has put a small red box at the corner of the block, and anyone can drop these things there free of charge. The box is often full by the end of the week, and the city weighs the whole box and tells him the number. He writes the number in a book, and a sudden rise in it makes him check the box for something wrong. His record for one week is a hundred and eighty kilograms, while in his first year it was less than fifty. He says this is the most useful thing he has ever done, not because the numbers are important, but because many neighbours still put a paper cup or a broken phone into the red box. He does not get angry. He takes out only a few wrong things and shows them to their owners at the next gate. Last year four hundred families learned to use the box correctly because of those short talks.
**Q1**
What does the old man do when he finds a paper cup in the red box?
- A. He puts it back into the red box.
- B. He writes it in his book with the weight.
- C. He takes it out and shows the owner.
- D. He leaves it at the gate for a week.
**Answer** C
**Q2**
What is the old man's record for one week now?
- A. Less than fifty kilograms.
- B. A hundred and eighty kilograms.
- C. About one hundred kilograms.
- D. More than four hundred kilograms.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
How many families learned to use the box correctly last year?
- A. About forty families.
- B. About ninety families.
- C. About nine hundred families.
- D. About four hundred families.
**Answer** D
**Q4**
What can we learn about the old man from the way he deals with wrong rubbish?
- A. He wants to show that he has done his job well.
- B. He would rather talk to people than simply get angry.
- C. The city pays him for every kilogram.
- D. His family wants him to collect more.
**Answer** B
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?
- A. How a city office measures the rubbish each year.
- B. How two cloth bags can be used every Wednesday.
- C. How an old man taught neighbours to sort rubbish.
- D. How old medicine should be given to a shop.
**Answer** C

## C3-03 十六次日出的地方
**Passage**
In a space station above the earth, the sun does not rise once a day. The station goes around the earth about sixteen times in twenty-four hours, so the sun goes up and goes down sixteen times as well. People who stay there say the first few weeks are hard, because the body never gets a real night to rest in. They sleep with their sleeping bags fixed to a wall, or they would float away when they turn over. They do not pour water from a jug either. Every drop is packed in a small bag, and the crew drinks it through a straw. The same is true of food: it is never served on a plate, because anything loose would slowly float to the ceiling. Fresh fruit and warm bread are the things they miss most. When a supply ship arrives with fresh food and water, the crew first count the boxes together and then sit down to eat at the same time. It is one of the quietest meals in space, and nobody leaves the table until everyone has finished.
**Q1**
How often do the people in the station see a sunrise?
- A. About once every three hours.
- B. About twice in twenty-four hours.
- C. About once every four hours.
- D. About once every ninety minutes.
**Answer** D
**Q2**
How do they drink water in the station?
- A. From an open cup on a table.
- B. From a small bag through a straw.
- C. From a bottle with a wide mouth.
- D. From a pipe fixed to the wall.
**Answer** B
**Q3**
Why can the crew never serve food on a plate?
- A. Because loose things would float to the ceiling.
- B. Because there is no table in the station.
- C. Because the crew eats much faster than usual.
- D. Because the plates cannot be washed in space.
**Answer** A
**Q4**
What can we learn about the crew's meal after a supply ship arrives?
- A. They always eat alone in their own rooms.
- B. They eat only after the boxes are empty.
- C. They eat together and wait for one another.
- D. They eat in a hurry to save the food.
**Answer** C
**Q5**
What is the passage mainly about?

## C3-04 拔掉的插头
**Passage**
Many people believe that a machine which is not in use takes no electricity. That is not true. A television, a computer and a small TV box all keep a little electricity going, so that they can answer at once when you press a button. The set is waiting for a signal from the remote control, the computer slowly goes to sleep, and the little box keeps a light burning all night. Last July, during the summer holiday, a family in Building Six did a simple test. On Monday morning they pulled out the plug of every machine in the living room except the fridge. The fridge was left alone because it makes its own cold and keeps the food safe. One week later the family had used one unit less than in the week before. That one unit was enough to run a rice cooker for two hours. The family had expected a much bigger number, so the father went back through the old bills in the box and found that the numbers matched.
**Q1**

- A. The plug of every machine but the fridge.

- D. The old meter on the ground floor.
**Answer** A
**Q2**

- A. Ten units less than the week before.
- B. Exactly the same as the week before.
- C. One unit less than the week before.
- D. One unit more than the week before.
**Answer** C
**Q3**
Why do these machines keep a little electricity going?
- A. So that they use more power when they are working.
- B. So that they can answer at once when you press a button.

What can we learn about the family in Building Six?

**Q5**

## C3-05 树荫下的地面
**Passage**
On the hottest afternoon of last July, a science club of Class Two put one thermometer in each of four places around the school: under a big tree, on the open playground, beside the wall of the teaching building, and on a small grass field. They read the four thermometers at one o'clock, two o'clock, three o'clock and four o'clock, and they wrote the numbers down. At three o'clock the playground was eleven degrees hotter than the ground under the tree. The place beside the wall was cooler than the playground, but it was still four degrees warmer than the tree. The grass field was only two degrees warmer than the tree. Then the teacher showed them how a tree cools the ground. The roots take water from the ground and send it up to the leaves, and the water leaves the leaves as a gas. When water turns into gas, it takes heat away from the leaf and from the ground around it. That is why the air under a tree stays cool. Nobody in the club had known this before. Now they measure the same four places every May, and they have asked the school to plant three young trees on both sides of the playground.
**Q1**

- A. Two degrees.
- B. Seven degrees.
- C. Eleven degrees.
- D. Four degrees.
**Answer** C
**Q2**

- B. Once, at one o'clock in the afternoon.
- C. Twice, in the morning and in the evening.
- D. Every half hour from ten to four.
**Answer** A
**Q3**

**Answer** B

**Answer** B
**Q5**

**Answer** C
